## Fact check

- Tinderboxes held flint, a steel striker, tinder (often char cloth) and sulfur-tipped splints; striking steel on flint shears off hot iron particles (sparks).
- A spark caught on char cloth forms an ember, which is blown up and used to light a sulfur splint; it could take minutes, especially in damp conditions.
- John Walker, an English chemist, sold the first friction matches in 1826/1827.
- Safety matches (Swedish, Lundström) were developed and spread in the 1850s.
