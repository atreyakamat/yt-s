## Fact check

- Mayonnaise is an oil-in-water emulsion; commercial mayo is typically about 70-80% oil.
- Oil and water separate because they're immiscible.
- Egg yolk contains emulsifiers, notably lecithin (a phospholipid with a water-loving head and oil-loving tail), which coat droplets and stop them merging.
- The very high fraction of tightly packed droplets gives mayonnaise its thick, spoonable texture.
- Adding oil too fast can make the emulsion 'split'.
