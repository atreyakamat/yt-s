## Fact check

- Low-pressure sodium (LPS) lamps contain sodium metal and a neon-argon starting gas; on start-up they glow pinkish-red from the neon until the sodium vaporises.
- LPS light is almost monochromatic at about 589 nm (yellow-orange), so colour rendering is essentially nil and coloured objects appear in shades of grey/orange.
- LPS lamps were among the most efficient light sources (high lumens per watt); they appeared in the 1930s and are now largely replaced by white LEDs.
