## Fact check

- A 2017 UC Berkeley study (Proceedings of the Royal Society A) filmed shoelace knots on a runner on a treadmill with slow-motion cameras.
- They measured about 7 g of acceleration at the knot on foot strike, which loosens it, while leg swing produces inertial pulling on the free ends.
- Knots held for many strides, then failed rapidly within a couple of strides once loosening began ('avalanche').
- The strong form of the bow (based on a square knot) lies across the shoe and outlasted the weak (granny) form, which tends to twist along the shoe.
- About 7 g at each footstrike: reported in Oxland et al. 2017 (Proc. R. Soc. A); paper page not retrievable, left as 'about'. Source: https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rspa.2017.0100
