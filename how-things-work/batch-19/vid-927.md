## Fact check

- Freezing needs nucleation sites (impurities, rough surfaces, bubbles); without them, pure still water can be supercooled several degrees below 0 °C (home-freezer demonstrations typically reach around -5 to -10 °C; -8 is shown as a typical value).
- A tap or shock (or contact with ice) triggers nucleation, and crystals spread through the water within seconds.
- Freezing releases latent heat, warming the mixture to 0 °C, so only part freezes, forming slush.
- Pouring supercooled water onto an ice cube freezes it on contact, building an ice tower.
- Pure water can supercool well below 0 C, -8 C within range; freezes when disturbed: OK. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercooling
