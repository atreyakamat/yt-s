## Fact check

- Commercial airliners are struck by lightning on average about once a year (commonly cited figure).
- Many strikes are aircraft-initiated as the plane flies through charged regions.
- Strikes typically attach at extremities (nose, wingtips) and exit from another (often the tail).
- The conductive skin acts like a Faraday cage; composite aircraft include embedded metal mesh/foil; fuel systems are designed to avoid sparks.
