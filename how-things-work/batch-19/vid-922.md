## Fact check

- Cored solder wire contains one or more cores of flux (commonly rosin-based) inside a tube of solder alloy.
- Flux removes oxide films and prevents re-oxidation so molten solder can wet the metal.
- 63/37 tin-lead is eutectic and melts at 183 °C at a single temperature; common lead-free SAC alloys melt around 217-221 °C over a small range; non-eutectic alloys have a pasty range.
- Visible soldering fumes are mainly from the flux.
- Dull, lumpy joints are a sign of a 'cold joint' in tin-lead soldering (lead-free joints can look less shiny normally).
- 63/37 eutectic melts at 183 C; lead-free about 217 C and up (script says 220, rounded): OK. Flux removes oxide: OK. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solder
