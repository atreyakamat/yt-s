## Fact check

- Basic rice cookers have a heating plate and a spring-loaded thermostat under the pot containing a permanent magnet and a ferrite element.
- While free water remains, the pot stays near 100 °C. Once it is absorbed or boiled off, the temperature rises.
- The ferrite loses its magnetism above its Curie temperature (about 103 °C in these devices), releasing the magnet; a spring drops the lever and the cooker switches to keep-warm.
- Advanced 'fuzzy logic' cookers use electronic sensors instead, hence 'simple cookers'.
