## Fact check

- Natural-draught cooling towers cool condenser water by spraying it through rising air; evaporation of a small part removes heat, and the visible plume is condensed water vapour, not smoke.
- Warm, moist air is less dense, so it rises through the tall shell without fans (natural draught).
- The hyperboloid is a doubly ruled surface, so it can be reinforced with straight bars; the shape is strong against wind and needs relatively little material, giving thin concrete shells.
- The first hyperboloid cooling towers were built in 1918 in the Netherlands (designed by Frederik van Iterson and Gerard Kuypers).
