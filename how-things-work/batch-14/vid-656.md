## Fact check

- Slow cookers have a heating element in the outer metal housing (often around the sides) and a removable heavy stoneware/ceramic pot with a lid.
- Typical slow cookers draw roughly 150-300 W; electric kettles draw about 1,500-3,000 W, so the bars (200 vs 2,000 W) are representative.
- Contents generally reach about 80-100 °C (low setting below a full boil); an unpressurised pot cannot heat water-based food above about 100 °C.
- Long cooking converts collagen in connective tissue to gelatin, tenderising tough cuts.
