## Fact check

- Engelbart's 1960s NLS used an upward-pointing arrow cursor.
- On early low-resolution bitmap displays (e.g. Xerox Alto, 1970s), the cursor was tilted; the usual explanation is that a tilted arrow has one vertical edge that renders cleanly, while an upright arrow has two diagonal, jagged edges.
- The cursor's hotspot is its tip, at the image's top-left corner, which suits screen coordinates that start at the top left.
- The design persisted out of familiarity.
- Engelbart's original cursor was an upward-pointing arrow; slanted on the low-resolution PARC machines: OK. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Engelbart
