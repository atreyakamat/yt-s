## Fact check

- On standard six-sided dice, opposite faces sum to seven (1-6, 2-5, 3-4), so 6 and 1 are never adjacent.
- Since 1, 2 and 3 are mutually non-opposite, they meet at one corner, arranged either clockwise or anticlockwise; the two possible dice are mirror images.
- The sum-to-seven convention is ancient and appears on at least some dice from antiquity (e.g. Mohenjo-daro, about 2500-1900 BCE), though early dice were not always consistent.
- Knowing the top face gives the bottom as 7 minus the top.
- Opposite faces sum to 7; 1, 2, 3 share a corner; two mirror-image (left/right-handed) dice: OK. Ancient dice: at least one Mohenjo-daro die follows it, so softened to 'some'. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dice
