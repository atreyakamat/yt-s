## Fact check

- Brick and mortar are porous; moisture rises by capillary action (rising damp), typically no more than about 1-1.5 m.
- A damp proof course is a horizontal impermeable layer built into the wall near ground level.
- Victorian DPCs were often slate or bitumen; modern ones are usually polyethylene.
- UK guidance places the DPC at least 150 mm above external ground level to prevent splash-back bridging it.
- Damp-proof course about 150-200 mm above ground: OK. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damp_proofing
