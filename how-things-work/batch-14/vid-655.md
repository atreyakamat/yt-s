## Fact check

- Tympanic (ear) thermometers measure infrared radiation emitted by the eardrum and ear canal using a thermopile or similar sensor behind an IR-transparent window; tips use disposable covers.
- The ear canal forms a nearly enclosed cavity, which helps it behave like a good infrared emitter.
- The eardrum shares blood supply with regions near the hypothalamus, so it tracks core temperature reasonably well.
- Poor positioning that views the cooler canal wall gives low readings; readings take about a second. Educational only.
