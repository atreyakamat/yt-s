## Fact check

- Fats, oils and grease solidify in cool sewers and combine with non-flushable wet wipes to form fatbergs.
- Fats react with calcium in water to form calcium soaps (saponification), producing a hard, soap-like mass.
- The 2017 Whitechapel fatberg in east London weighed about 130 tonnes and was about 250 m long; removal took weeks using high-pressure jets and hand tools.
- Water companies advise flushing only pee, poo and paper.
