## Fact check

- Before electric kettles, kettles were heated on hearths and stoves.
- Electric kettles were sold from the 1890s (e.g. by Crompton & Co. in the UK and Carpenter Electric in the US); their heating element sat in a separate compartment under the water, making them slow.
- 1922: Arthur Leslie Large (Bulpitt & Sons) introduced an element sealed in a metal tube immersed in the water.
- 1955: the first kettle with an automatic steam-operated bimetallic switch-off was introduced in the UK (Russell Hobbs K1).
- Early electric kettles (GE 1890, Carpenter 1891) had a separate heating compartment and were inefficient; 1922 element sealed in a tube inside the water (Leslie Large); 1955 Russell Hobbs K1 steam-operated bimetal switch-off: OK. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_kettle
