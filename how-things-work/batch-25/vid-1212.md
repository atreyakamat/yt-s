## Fact check

- Natural cork is the bark of the cork oak (Quercus suber); it's stripped by hand without felling the tree and regrows, harvested about every nine years.
- Cork tissue consists of many tiny, closed, air-filled cells (tens of millions per cubic centimetre), making it compressible and resilient.
- Cell walls contain suberin, a waxy, water-repellent substance.
- Robert Hooke coined the term 'cell' in Micrographia (1665) after observing cork.
- Cork is harvested about every nine years: widely cited (Cork oak); Hooke named cells in 1665 (Micrographia). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_(biology)
