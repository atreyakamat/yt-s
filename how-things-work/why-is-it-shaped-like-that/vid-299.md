## Fact check

- Plain and interlocking roof tiles are hung on timber battens using nibs and/or nails, usually over an underlay membrane.
- Each course overlaps the course below (headlap), and courses are laid broken-bond (staggered by half a tile) so vertical joints don't align; water from a joint lands on the middle of the tile below.
- Steeper pitches shed water faster; tile manufacturers specify minimum pitches for this reason.
