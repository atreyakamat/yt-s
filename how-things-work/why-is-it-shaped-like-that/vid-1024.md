## Fact check

- The diamond frame consists of a front triangle (top, down and seat tubes) and a rear triangle (seat stays, chain stays) sharing the seat tube.
- Triangles are geometrically rigid: their shape is fixed by the side lengths, while a four-sided frame can shear unless its joints resist bending.
- This lets members carry loads mostly in tension/compression, allowing light tubing.
- The diamond frame became standard on safety bicycles in the late 1880s-1890s and remains dominant.
