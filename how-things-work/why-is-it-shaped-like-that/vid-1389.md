## Fact check

- A soap bubble is a thin water film sandwiched between layers of soap molecules, enclosing air.
- Surface tension pulls the film to minimize its area.
- For a fixed volume, a sphere has the least surface area; a cube of equal volume has about 24% more surface.
- So free bubbles become spherical regardless of the wand's shape.
- The colors come from thin-film interference between light reflected from the outer and inner surfaces; bubbles pop when drainage and evaporation thin the film too far.
