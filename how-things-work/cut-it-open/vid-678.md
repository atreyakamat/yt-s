## Fact check

- A turbocharger has an exhaust-driven turbine and an intake compressor on a common shaft; more air allows more fuel to be burned for more power.
- Car turbos commonly spin at 100,000-250,000 rpm.
- Compression heats intake air; an intercooler cools it to increase density.
- Turbo lag: delay while exhaust flow builds to spin the turbine up to boost speed.
