## Fact check

- A pea whistle makes sound when the air jet hits a sharp edge, setting the air in the chamber resonating.
- The chamber contains a small ball ('pea'), traditionally cork, which the airflow spins around.
- As the pea moves, it periodically disrupts the airflow, modulating the tone into the familiar trill.
- The warbling, modulated sound is widely used by referees because it stands out from crowd noise; pealess designs also exist.
