## Fact check

- A classic kaleidoscope has two or three long mirrors in the tube, often three forming a triangle, with an object chamber of loose beads or glass at the end.
- Light reflects repeatedly between the mirrors, creating mirrored copies of the object chamber.
- Mirrors at 60 degrees produce a pattern with six-fold symmetry around the center.
- Loose pieces shift on turning, so the patterns rarely repeat.
- Sir David Brewster invented the kaleidoscope in 1816 and patented it in 1817.
