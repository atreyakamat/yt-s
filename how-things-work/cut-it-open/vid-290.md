## Fact check

- Britain's transmission grid runs at 400 kV and 275 kV, so the largest substations take in 400,000 volts (other countries use similar or higher voltages).
- Higher voltage means lower current for the same power, so less energy is lost as heat in the lines.
- UK distribution steps down through 132/33/11 kV to 400 V three-phase (230 V single-phase) for streets, so the bars are representative values.
- Substations contain transformers (oil-filled, with cooling fins/radiators), circuit breakers that interrupt faults within a fraction of a second, and busbars that distribute power to outgoing feeders.
