## Fact check

- Fountain pen feeds use capillary channels to deliver ink to the nib slit.
- Air must replace ink leaving the reservoir; feeds include an air channel so air bubbles enter as ink flows out.
- Comb-like fins on the feed buffer excess ink pushed out when air in the reservoir expands (e.g. from hand warmth).
- Nibs have a slit; pressure spreads the tines and widens the line, more so on flexible nibs.
