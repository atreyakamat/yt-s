## Fact check

- Induction hobs have a flat copper coil under a glass-ceramic top, driven by power electronics.
- Typical operating frequency is about 20-100 kHz, i.e. tens of thousands of cycles per second.
- The alternating magnetic field induces eddy currents (plus magnetic hysteresis losses) in a ferromagnetic pan base, heating the pan directly.
- Most induction hobs need ferromagnetic cookware (a magnet sticks to the base); plain aluminium, copper or glass will not work unless they have a magnetic base plate.
- The glass is heated only indirectly by the hot pan, and the hob stops heating when the pan is removed.
