## Fact check

- Vapour-compression refrigeration: a refrigerant evaporates in the evaporator inside the fridge (absorbing heat), is compressed (getting hot), condenses in the condenser coils outside (releasing heat), then passes an expansion device (capillary tube or valve) where pressure and temperature drop.
- The refrigerant boils at a low temperature at the low pressure inside the evaporator.
- Condenser coils are on the back or built into the sides of many fridges, which is why they feel warm.
