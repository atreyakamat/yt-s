## Fact check

- NAND flash stores data as charge on a floating gate or charge-trap layer isolated by oxide insulators; charge persists without power.
- Programming uses high voltage to tunnel electrons (Fowler-Nordheim tunnelling) through the insulator; erasing removes them.
- Stored charge shifts the transistor's threshold voltage, which is read as bit values; cells can store 1-4 bits (SLC to QLC).
- Program/erase cycles degrade the oxide, giving flash finite endurance.
