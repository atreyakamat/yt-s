## Fact check

- Wetsuits are made from neoprene foam, a synthetic rubber containing many small closed gas cells (often nitrogen), usually laminated with nylon fabric.
- The gas cells provide insulation.
- A thin layer of water gets in; the body warms it, and a snug fit limits flushing with fresh cold water.
- Thickness is in millimeters; common suits run from about 2 mm for warm water to 5 mm or more for cold.
- At depth, water pressure compresses the neoprene's gas cells, reducing thickness, insulation and buoyancy.
