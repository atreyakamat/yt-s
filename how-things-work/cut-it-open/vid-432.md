## Fact check

- Carbonated drinks contain dissolved carbon dioxide.
- Henry's law: the amount of gas dissolved is proportional to its pressure, so carbonating under pressure dissolves much more CO2.
- Gas solubility falls as temperature rises; CO2 is roughly twice as soluble near 0 °C as near 25 °C (bars are a relative illustration, about 3.3 g/L vs 1.5 g/L).
- Commercial carbonation chills the liquid, injects CO2 under pressure and seals the container.
- A small fraction of dissolved CO2 forms carbonic acid, adding a tangy 'bite'.
