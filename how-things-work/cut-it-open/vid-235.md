## Fact check

- Alcohol-based hand rubs typically contain about 60-95% ethanol or isopropanol plus water, a gelling agent and emollients such as glycerin. Public health agencies recommend at least 60% alcohol.
- Alcohol disrupts lipid membranes and denatures proteins; water is needed for effective protein denaturation, so 60-90% solutions outperform near-pure alcohol.
- Coverage and contact time matter, so rubbing until dry is standard guidance.
- Alcohol is less effective against some germs, such as norovirus and bacterial spores, hence 'not every germ'.
