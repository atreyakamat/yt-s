## Fact check

- Bread machines have a removable tin with a kneading paddle driven from below by a motor and belt, and a heating element around the tin, run by a programmed timer.
- Kneading develops gluten; during rising the machine keeps the dough warm (around 30 °C) while yeast produces carbon dioxide, which gluten traps.
- The loaf is then baked in the same tin (roughly 160-190 °C; 180 °C shown as typical).
- The paddle stays in the dough during baking and often leaves a hole in the base of the loaf.
