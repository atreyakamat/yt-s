## Fact check

- Smouldering wood with limited oxygen produces smoke rather than flame.
- Wood smoke contains many compounds (phenols, acids, carbonyls) with antimicrobial and antioxidant effects.
- Antioxidant phenols slow fat oxidation (rancidity).
- Smoking also dries the food surface, which inhibits microbes.
- Traditional preservation combined salting with prolonged smoking; modern lightly smoked foods rely on refrigeration and smoking mainly adds flavour.
