## Fact check

- Wool fibres have overlapping cuticle scales and a natural crimp (waviness) that traps air.
- The fibre surface is water-repellent while the cortex absorbs water vapour.
- Wool can absorb roughly 30% of its dry weight in moisture before feeling wet: widely quoted by wool research bodies.
- Absorbing water vapour releases heat (heat of sorption): documented property of wool.
