## Fact check

- Herman Snellen introduced his chart in 1862 using specially designed letters (optotypes).
- Snellen optotypes are drawn on a 5×5 grid; stroke width and gap equal one-fifth of the letter height.
- On the 20/20 (6/6) line, the whole letter subtends 5 arcminutes and each stroke 1 arcminute at the test distance.
- 20/40 means you need to be at 20 ft to read what a typical eye reads at 40 ft. Charts use a limited set of letters chosen for similar legibility; LogMAR charts came in the 1970s.
