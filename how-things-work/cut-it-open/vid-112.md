## Fact check

- A digital multimeter's core is an analogue-to-digital converter that measures voltage.
- Voltage ranges have a very high input resistance (commonly about 10 megohms) so they draw almost no current.
- Current is measured by passing it through a low-value shunt resistor and measuring the voltage drop (Ohm's law); the current input is fused.
- Resistance is measured by passing a known small current through the part and measuring the voltage.
- Continuity mode beeps below a low resistance threshold.
