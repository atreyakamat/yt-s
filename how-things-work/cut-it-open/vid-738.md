## Fact check

- Speedometers derive speed from wheel or transmission rotation (now via electronic sensors).
- Eddy-current speedometers use a rotating magnet that induces currents in an aluminium cup, dragging it against a hairspring to move the needle.
- UN ECE Regulation 39 (Europe and other UNECE countries) requires indicated speed never to be below true speed, allowing over-reading of up to 10 % + 4 km/h; makers calibrate slightly high to stay compliant as tyres vary. The 67/70 bars are an example, not a measurement.
- GPS speed is generally accurate at steady speeds.
