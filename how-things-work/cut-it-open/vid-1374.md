## Fact check

- A vibrating string has a tiny cross-section and radiates very little sound on its own.
- On an acoustic guitar, vibration passes through the saddle and bridge into the soundboard (top), which radiates sound efficiently.
- Tops are commonly braced, often in an X pattern, to resist string tension while still vibrating.
- The air in the body and the sound hole form a Helmholtz resonator (like blowing across a bottle), reinforcing low frequencies.
- Solid-body electric guitars are quiet unplugged because they lack a resonant soundboard and body cavity.
