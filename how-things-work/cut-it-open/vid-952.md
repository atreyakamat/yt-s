## Fact check

- Common white/yellow wood glue is a PVA (polyvinyl acetate) emulsion: polymer particles dispersed in water.
- Water is absorbed by the wood and evaporates; the particles coalesce into a film that has penetrated the fibres. It dries from white to translucent.
- Clamping gives a thin, continuous glue line; thick glue lines are weaker.
- Properly glued PVA joints in wood typically fail in the wood rather than the glue line (manufacturer claims and standard tests).
- Typical guidance: clamp about 30-60 minutes; full cure about 24 hours.
