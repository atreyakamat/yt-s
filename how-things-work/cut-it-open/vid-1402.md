## Fact check

- Hot-dip galvanising immerses cleaned steel in molten zinc at about 450 °C; a metallurgically bonded coating of zinc-iron alloy layers topped with zinc forms.
- Zinc is more electrochemically active than iron, so it corrodes preferentially (sacrificial/cathodic protection), protecting exposed steel at small scratches and cut edges.
- Spangle is the visible zinc crystal pattern on some galvanised surfaces.
- Zinc corrodes slowly, forming a protective patina; hot-dip coatings commonly last decades in many outdoor environments.
