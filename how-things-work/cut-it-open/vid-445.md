## Fact check

- Clinical MRI magnets are typically 1.5 or 3 tesla; Earth's field is about 25-65 microtesla, so 1.5 T is roughly 30,000 times stronger.
- The main magnet is superconducting and cooled with liquid helium to about 4 K.
- MRI detects signals from hydrogen nuclei (mostly in water and fat) after radiofrequency excitation as they relax.
- Gradient coils switching rapidly produce the loud knocking; MRI uses no ionising radiation.
- Bars show relative brightness on a typical T1-weighted image, illustrative only; cortical bone gives little signal.
