## Fact check

- Gas mantles are fabric mesh bags impregnated with metal salts (historically thorium and cerium, now often yttrium-based).
- On first lighting the fabric burns away, leaving a brittle ceramic mesh of metal oxides.
- Heated by the flame, the oxides glow brightly white, far brighter than the flame itself.
- Used mantles are extremely fragile and crumble when touched.
- Carl Auer von Welsbach developed the gas mantle in the 1880s; mantles were widely used in gas street lighting before electric lighting dominated.
