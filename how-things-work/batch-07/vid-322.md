## Fact check

- Water-based (latex/emulsion) paints are pigment plus a binder of tiny acrylic or vinyl-acrylic polymer particles dispersed in water, with additives.
- Drying happens by water evaporating; the particles pack together and coalesce into a continuous film, after which the paint no longer washes off with water.
- Titanium dioxide is the main white pigment; its high refractive index scatters light, giving hiding power.
- Below the minimum film-forming temperature the particles don't coalesce properly; paint tins state a minimum application temperature.
