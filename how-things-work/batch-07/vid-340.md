## Fact check

- X-ray tubes heat a filament (cathode) to emit electrons, accelerate them with a high voltage, and stop them in a metal anode, often tungsten, producing X-rays.
- About 1% of the electron energy becomes X-rays; roughly 99% becomes heat. Rotating anodes spread the heat.
- Typical chest X-ray exposures last a small fraction of a second (milliseconds to tens of milliseconds).
- Bone contains calcium and absorbs more X-rays than soft tissue, so it appears white.
