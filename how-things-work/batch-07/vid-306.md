## Fact check

- Reeded (milled) edges were introduced to deter clipping and filing of precious-metal coins; a damaged edge is easy to see.
- Machine-made coins with milled edges were produced in England from the 1600s; Isaac Newton became Warden of the Royal Mint in 1696 and prosecuted counterfeiters and clippers.
- Edge differences (smooth vs reeded) help people, including blind and partially sighted people, identify coins by touch, e.g. the US dime is reeded and the cent is smooth.
- Milled edges (1600s) and Newton at the Mint from 1696: OK, Newton oversaw the recoinage rather than inventing milled edges. Source: https://www.perthmint.com/news/collector/coin-collecting/how-isaac-newton-helped-shape-our-coins/
