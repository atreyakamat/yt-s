## Fact check

- HTTPS (TLS) gives encryption in transit and authenticates the server's domain via a certificate.
- TLS key exchange (e.g. Diffie-Hellman) lets two parties agree on a secret over a public network.
- Certificates are signed by certificate authorities trusted by the browser.
- Free domain-validated certificates are easy to get, and many phishing sites use HTTPS; the padlock does not vouch for the site's honesty. Several browsers removed or changed the padlock icon partly for this reason.
