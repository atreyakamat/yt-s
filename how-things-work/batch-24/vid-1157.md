## Fact check

- Geostationary satellites orbit at 35,786 km above the equator, completing one orbit per day, so they appear fixed.
- A request/response via GEO travels user-satellite-ground station and back: about 4 × 35,786 ≈ 143,000 km, ~480 ms at light speed; real latency ~600 ms.
- Low Earth orbit constellations use thousands of satellites around 550 km up, with typical latency of roughly 25-60 ms.
- LEO satellites cross the sky in minutes; user terminals use electronically steered phased-array antennas to switch between them.
- Fibre latency value is a typical example.
- GEO altitude about 35,786 km, ~550 ms ideal round trip; Starlink ~550 km: OK. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_internet_access
