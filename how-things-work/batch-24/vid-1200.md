## Fact check

- Roman fullers (fullones) worked in laundries called fullonicae, cleaning woollen clothing; remains survive at Pompeii and Ostia.
- They used stale urine, in which urea breaks down to ammonia, an alkali that helps remove grease and dirt; workers trod cloth in tubs (sometimes called the 'fuller's dance' or saltus fullonicus).
- Urine was collected from public vessels; Emperor Vespasian (1st century AD) taxed its collection. Suetonius reports he held a coin to his son Titus's nose and asked if it smelled, the origin of 'pecunia non olet'.
- Urine tax attributed to Vespasian ("money does not stink"): OK. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vespasian
