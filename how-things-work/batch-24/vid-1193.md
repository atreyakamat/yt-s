## Fact check

- The '40-50% of heat lost through the head' claim is commonly traced to 1950s US military cold tests in which subjects wore arctic suits but no hat.
- The head is roughly 7-10% of body surface area and loses heat roughly in proportion (BMJ, Pretorius & Tipton, 2008).
- Scalp blood vessels constrict less in cold than those in hands and feet, so an uncovered head keeps losing heat.
- When the body is insulated, an uncovered head is a relatively large share of heat loss, so hats help.
