# vid-1624 · Why stars twinkle but planets don't

## Hook

- **Chosen (0-3s):** "Stars twinkle. Planets mostly don't."
- Backup 1: "A way to tell planets from stars."
- Backup 2: "Why stars blink."

## Fact check (verify before posting)

Atmospheric scintillation (turbulence refracts light); stars are effectively point sources, planets have small angular diameters (few to tens of arcseconds), averaging out the variations. Planets can twinkle near the horizon.

## Voiceover (read-through)

1. Stars twinkle. Planets mostly don't.
2. Starlight crosses turbulent air on the way down.
3. Stars are so far they look like single points.
4. Planets look like tiny discs, not points.
5. The air bends their light less visibly overall.
6. Astronomers call twinkling scintillation.
