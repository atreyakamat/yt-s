## Fact check (verify before posting)

Lunar New Year falls on the new moon between 21 Jan and 20 Feb (typically the second new moon after the winter solstice).

## Voiceover (read-through)

1. Chinese New Year lands on a different date.
2. It follows a lunisolar calendar, tied to the Moon.
3. It starts at the second new moon after winter solstice, usually.
4. It falls between about January 21 and February 20.
5. Each year has an animal from a twelve-year cycle.
6. Families gather and spring clean first.
