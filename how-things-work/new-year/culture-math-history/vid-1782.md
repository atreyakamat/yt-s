# vid-1782 · Why zero was a breakthrough

## Hook

- **Chosen (0-3s):** "Zero was one of maths' great inventions."
- Backup 1: "The number that took centuries to accept."
- Backup 2: "From placeholder to number."

## Fact check (verify before posting)

Brahmagupta's Brahmasphutasiddhanta (628 CE) gave rules for zero as a number; Bakhshali manuscript (radiocarbon dated to as early as ~3rd-4th c. CE) uses a dot placeholder; Fibonacci's Liber Abaci (1202) popularised Hindu-Arabic numerals in Europe.

## Voiceover (read-through)

1. Zero was one of maths' great inventions.
2. Ancient Babylonians used a placeholder mark.
3. Indian mathematicians treated zero as a number.
4. Brahmagupta wrote rules for it in 628 CE.
5. It spread to Europe through Arabic texts.
6. Place value made arithmetic far easier.
