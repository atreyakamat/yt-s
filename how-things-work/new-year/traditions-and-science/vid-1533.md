# vid-1533 · The clocks that keep GPS honest

## Hook

- **Chosen (0-3s):** "GPS must correct for Einstein every day."
- Backup 1: "Your maps work because of relativity."
- Backup 2: "Satellites run 38 microseconds fast."

## Fact check (verify before posting)

GPS satellite clocks gain ~45.7 microseconds/day from GR and lose ~7.2 from SR: net ~38.5 µs/day; uncorrected would give ~10 km/day position error (Ashby, Living Reviews in Relativity).

## Voiceover (read-through)

1. GPS must correct for Einstein every day.
2. Satellite clocks tick slightly faster than ours on the ground.
3. Weaker gravity speeds them up, and their motion slows them down.
4. Net result: about thirty-eight microseconds fast every day.
5. Uncorrected, position errors would grow by kilometres daily.
6. So engineers tune the clocks to cancel it.
