## Fact check

- Velocipedes and high-wheelers had pedals attached directly to the front wheel hub (direct drive).
- Distance per pedal revolution equals the wheel's circumference: a 1.4-1.5 m diameter wheel covers about 4.5 m; a ~0.65 m wheel about 2 m.
- High-wheelers (penny-farthings) were popular in the 1870s and 1880s, with wheel size limited by the rider's leg length.
- Riders sat high and forward, so sudden stops caused 'headers' over the handlebars.
- The chain-driven Rover safety bicycle (J. K. Starley, 1885) used gearing to allow smaller wheels.
- Giant front wheels 1870s; Rover safety bicycle 1885; headers: OK. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penny-farthing
