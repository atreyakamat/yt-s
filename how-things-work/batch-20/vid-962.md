## Fact check

- 'Cut and paste' originally referred to physically cutting and pasting manuscript text.
- Larry Tesler and colleagues at Xerox PARC developed computer cut/copy/paste in the mid-1970s (Gypsy editor).
- Apple's Lisa and Macintosh (early 1980s) popularised the Z/X/C/V shortcuts; X resembles scissors, V is often described as an insertion mark.
- The clipboard is temporary storage for copied data.
- 'Billions a day' is a reasonable estimate for global usage rather than a measured figure; gauge is illustrative.
- Cut/copy/paste at Xerox PARC from 1974 (Tesler); X/C/V shortcuts on Lisa 1983, Mac 1984: OK. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cut,_copy,_and_paste
