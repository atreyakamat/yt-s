## Fact check

- Humidity slows sweat evaporation so heat stress rises; heat index combines air temperature and relative humidity into a feels-like value; example 32 C (90 F) at 70% relative humidity feels like 41 C (106 F); index assumes shade, direct sunlight can add up to 8 C (14 F), wind and activity not included; Robert G. Steadman 1979. 'Used in heat warnings' is described on the NWS table (colour-coded risk levels). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_index
