## Fact check

- The knocker-up's job was rousing sleeping people so they could get to work on time, during the Industrial Revolution when alarm clocks were unreliable and expensive; paid a few pence a week. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knocker-up
- Tools: short heavy baton for doors, long light stick (often bamboo) for upper windows; Mary Smith in East London was photographed in 1931 using a pea-shooter. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knocker-up
- Ferryhill, County Durham used knocky-up boards (slate boards on miners' houses) so colliery knocker-ups knew when to wake each miner. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knocker-up
- The job mostly disappeared by the 1940s and 1950s but continued in some pockets of industrial England until the early 1970s. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knocker-up
- Ctesibius (active c. 285-222 BCE) added alarms to water clocks that dropped pebbles on a gong or blew trumpets; Levi Hutchins made the first American alarm clock in 1787 (rang at 4 a.m., for himself); Antoine Redier received the first patent for an adjustable mechanical alarm clock in 1847. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alarm_clock
- A mainspring powers a gear that moves a hammer back and forth between two bells. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alarm_clock
