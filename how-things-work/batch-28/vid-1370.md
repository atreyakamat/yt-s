## Fact check

- Mechanical sirens use a motor-driven rotor inside a stator with ports; air is thrown outward and interrupted as rotor and stator openings align, producing pressure pulses.
- Pitch equals ports passing per second (hundreds per second gives a tone of hundreds of hertz); it rises as the rotor speeds up, giving the characteristic wail.
- Many outdoor warning sirens rotate to sweep sound around the area. Electronic sirens with speakers also exist; the script describes the classic mechanical type.
