## Fact check

- Common wire nails have a diamond point (four-sided pyramid) formed by gripper/cutter dies as the nail is cut from wire.
- A sharp point wedges fibres apart, which can split wood near edges and ends.
- Blunting the point (tapping it with a hammer) is a long-standing carpentry trick: the blunt tip crushes and shears fibres rather than wedging them.
- Ring-shank (annular) nails have much higher withdrawal resistance than smooth-shank nails; spiral (twisted) shanks rotate as driven.
