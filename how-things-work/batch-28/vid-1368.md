## Fact check

- Batteries store DC; AC charging uses the vehicle's onboard charger to rectify grid AC, limited to a few kW to ~22 kW.
- DC rapid chargers contain the AC-DC conversion in the charging unit and supply DC directly to the battery.
- Car and charger communicate (e.g. via CCS/ISO 15118 or CHAdeMO protocols) to agree voltage/current before energising.
- Charging power tapers at high state of charge, commonly from around 80 %, to protect the cells.
- Typical powers: home wallbox about 7 kW; many rapid chargers 50 kW; the fastest public chargers up to about 350 kW.
