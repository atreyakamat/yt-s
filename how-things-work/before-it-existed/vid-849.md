## Fact check

- Strings for bowed and plucked instruments were traditionally made of gut, usually sheep intestine, twisted, dried and polished.
- Despite the name 'catgut', the material did not come from cats; the origin of the name is uncertain.
- Gut is sensitive to humidity and goes out of tune easily.
- Gut supply problems around World War II led to nylon classical guitar strings, introduced in the 1940s.
- Steel strings came into use on guitars from the late 1800s/early 1900s; gut is still used in historical performance.
