## Fact check

- The earliest cars used oil/kerosene lamps similar to carriage lamps; acetylene (carbide) lamps, where water drips onto calcium carbide to produce acetylene gas, were common in the 1900s and had to be lit by hand.
- Electric headlights became practical in the 1910s as cars gained electrical systems (generators and batteries).
- Dipping/low beams followed in the 1910s-20s; halogen headlamps spread in the 1960s; LED headlights from the 2000s.
- A standard H4/H7 halogen bulb is about 55 W; LED headlight units draw much less (labelled 'roughly').
