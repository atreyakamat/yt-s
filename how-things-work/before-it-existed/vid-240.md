## Fact check

- Mangles (wringers) were hand-cranked machines with two rollers that squeezed water from wet laundry; common in homes in the 19th and early 20th centuries, with a screw or spring to set roller pressure.
- Early electric washing machines (wringer washers) had a powered wringer mounted on top, used into the mid-20th century.
- Spin cycles and spin dryers replaced wringers by flinging water out of a perforated drum.
- Mangles were also used to press flat linens such as sheets and tablecloths.
