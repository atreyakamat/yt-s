## Fact check

- Snow reflects most incoming UV (up to around 80-90%), and UV can cause photokeratitis ('snow blindness'), a sunburn of the cornea.
- Inuit and Yupik peoples made snow goggles of bone, ivory, antler or wood with narrow slits; examples about 2,000 years old exist.
- Narrow slits act like a pinhole, slightly increasing depth of field, similar to squinting.
- Smoky quartz panes used as glare shields are recorded in 12th-century China (Song dynasty).
- Mass-market sunglasses spread from around 1929-1930s.
