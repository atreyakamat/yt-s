## Fact check

- Before Bazalgette's system, London's waste went to cesspits and drains that emptied into the Thames, which also supplied much drinking water.
- Cholera epidemics in 1832, 1848-49 and 1853-54 killed tens of thousands of Londoners.
- The 'Great Stink' of the hot summer of 1858 overwhelmed the riverside Houses of Parliament and spurred legislation for new sewers.
- Joseph Bazalgette's intercepting sewers, largely complete by about 1875, carried waste east to outfalls downstream; he deliberately enlarged pipe sizes, and much of the network is still in use over 150 years later.
