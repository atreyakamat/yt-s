## Fact check

- The oldest known ice skates were made from animal leg bones (horse, cattle), flattened on one side and tied to the feet; research points to Finland/northern Europe thousands of years ago.
- Bone skates have no edge; users propelled themselves with a pole.
- Research (Formenti and Minetti, 2007) suggests bone skates were a practical way to save energy crossing frozen lakes in winter.
- The Dutch are credited with adding metal blades with edges to wooden skates in the Middle Ages, enabling leg-powered gliding and steering.
- All-steel skate blades became common in the 19th century.
