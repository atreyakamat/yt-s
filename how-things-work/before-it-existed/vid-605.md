## Fact check

- The first cash machine opened on 27 June 1967 at a bank branch in Enfield, north London, developed by John Shepherd-Barron's team.
- It used paper vouchers impregnated with carbon-14, a mildly radioactive substance, which the machine detected and matched against a code; the maximum withdrawal was £10.
- Shepherd-Barron said he planned six-digit PINs but chose four because his wife could only remember four digits.
- There are millions of ATMs worldwide today (roughly three million).
