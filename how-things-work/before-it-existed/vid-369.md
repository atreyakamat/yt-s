## Fact check

- In the 19th century, high-quality billiard balls were turned from elephant ivory, and each tusk yielded only a small number of balls.
- Ivory shortages led a US billiards supplier (Phelan and Collender) to offer a $10,000 prize for a substitute in the 1860s.
- John Wesley Hyatt developed celluloid around 1869-1870, one of the first commercial plastics, and used it for billiard balls.
- Celluloid is highly flammable; later balls moved to other plastics and resins.
- Modern premium balls are cast phenolic resin, which is very durable.
