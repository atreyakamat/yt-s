## Fact check

- Experimental and aftermarket car radios existed in the 1920s; ignition systems caused severe radio interference, solved with suppressors.
- In 1930 the Galvin Manufacturing Corporation launched one of the first commercially successful car radios (the brand later named Motorola); it cost over $100 when a new car cost roughly $500-650.
- In the early 1930s several US jurisdictions debated banning car radios as a distraction; the bans did not take hold.
- Car radios became common equipment by the 1950s.
