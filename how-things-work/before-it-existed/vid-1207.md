## Fact check

- Before standardisation, manufacturers used their own thread forms and pitches, so nuts and bolts were often made as matched pairs.
- Joseph Whitworth proposed a standard in 1841 with a 55° thread angle, rounded crests/roots and specified threads per inch.
- William Sellers proposed a 60° flat-crested thread in the USA in 1864, which became the US standard.
- Thread incompatibility caused supply problems between US and British forces in WWII; the Unified Thread Standard was agreed by the US, UK and Canada in 1948-49.
- ISO metric threads are designated M + nominal diameter in mm; M6 coarse pitch is 1.0 mm.
