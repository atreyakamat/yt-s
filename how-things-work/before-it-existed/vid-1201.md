## Fact check

- CRT televisions use an electron gun with a heated cathode emitting electrons, accelerated toward a phosphor-coated screen.
- Phosphors glow when struck; colour CRTs use red, green and blue phosphor dots/stripes behind a shadow mask.
- Deflection yokes (magnetic coils) scan the beam in a raster, line by line, typically 50 or 60 fields per second.
- Deflection angle limits required tube depth. Electronic TV broadcasts began in the 1930s; LCD/plasma flat panels overtook CRTs in the 2000s.
