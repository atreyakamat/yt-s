## Fact check

- Before the 1840 postal reforms, British postage was charged per sheet, so an envelope counted as an extra sheet; letters were usually folded and sealed.
- 'Letterlocking' (term coined by conservator Jana Dambrogio) describes folding, cutting, slitting and sealing letters to act as their own envelopes; researchers have catalogued many hundreds to thousands of variations.
- Envelopes became common after prepaid uniform postage (1840) and cheaper paper. Grid is illustrative.
