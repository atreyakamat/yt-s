## Fact check

- During WWII, Vesta Stoudt, an ordnance plant worker, wrote to President Roosevelt suggesting a waterproof cloth tape for ammunition boxes sealed with paper tape and wax; the tape was then produced for the military (widely documented).
- Duct tape is made of a polyethylene film, a cotton (or poly-cotton) mesh scrim, and a rubber-based adhesive; the mesh lets it tear straight by hand.
- It was army green in the war and silver later, when it was used on heating and air ducts; the 'duck tape' nickname origin is debated, so the script avoids it.
- A 1998 Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory study found ordinary duct tape failed at sealing ducts compared with other sealants.
