## Fact check

- Cavity walls (two leaves of masonry with a gap) became common in UK house building from the 1920s.
- The cavity's original purpose was to stop rain penetration: moisture through the outer leaf runs down the cavity face and drains out.
- Metal wall ties connect the two leaves.
- Cavity wall insulation (blown mineral fibre or polystyrene beads) is injected through drilled holes, usually in mortar joints, which are then filled.
