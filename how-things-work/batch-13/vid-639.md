## Fact check

- Bayer and Diaconis (1992) showed that about seven riffle shuffles are needed to bring a 52-card deck close to random.
- After only a few riffles, sequences from the original order remain detectable.
- Overhand shuffles mix much more slowly and need far more repetitions.
- There are 52! (about 8 x 10^67) possible orders, so a properly shuffled deck is almost certainly in an order never seen before.
- Bayer and Diaconis (1992): about seven riffle shuffles for 52 cards (some later work says six): OK as 'about seven'. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuffling
