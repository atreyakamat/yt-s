## Fact check

- Seasoning is a layer of polymerised (cross-linked) fat formed when thin films of oil are heated beyond their smoke point; it is black, hard and fairly non-stick.
- Cooking with fat adds to the seasoning over time.
- Bare cast iron rusts quickly when left wet; standard care advice is to wash briefly, dry immediately (often on the heat) and apply a thin coat of oil.
- Excess oil polymerises unevenly and becomes sticky, so thin coats are recommended.
