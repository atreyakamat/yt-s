## Fact check

- Heat alarms are recommended for kitchens and garages where smoke alarms give false alarms; they detect temperature, not smoke.
- Modern heat alarms use a thermistor; many domestic fixed-temperature heat alarms (e.g. UK Grade D types) operate at about 58 °C, and some also respond to a rapid rate of rise.
- Heat alarms respond later than smoke alarms, so they don't replace smoke alarms in living areas. Grid and bar values are illustrative.
