## Fact check

- Before rubberised fabric, people used wool, oiled or waxed cloth, and skins for rain protection.
- Charles Macintosh, a Scottish chemist, patented waterproof fabric in 1823: rubber dissolved in coal-tar naphtha between two layers of cloth.
- The mackintosh ('mac') raincoat is named after him.
- Early rubberised coats smelled and became stiff in cold and sticky in heat; vulcanised (heat-treated) rubber from the 1840s fixed this.
