## Fact check

- Cellulose sponges are made from wood-pulp cellulose (as viscose) mixed with sodium sulfate crystals and reinforcing fibres; heating sets the cellulose and the crystals melt/dissolve out, leaving pores. Crystal size sets pore size.
- Cellulose sponges absorb many times their dry weight in water via capillary action.
- Studies have found kitchen sponges carry very high bacterial loads because they stay damp, warm and food-soiled. Many other sponges are polyurethane foam instead.
