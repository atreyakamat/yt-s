## Fact check

- Polymer banknotes are printed on biaxially oriented polypropylene film with opacifying white layers, leaving clear windows.
- Australia issued the first polymer banknote in 1988; Canada began its polymer series in 2011; the Bank of England's first polymer note (the £5) entered circulation in 2016.
- The Bank of England stated polymer notes last at least 2.5 times longer than paper notes.
- Polymer does not absorb water, so notes typically survive a wash.
- Australia first polymer note 1988, Canada 2011, UK 2016: OK. About 2.5x paper life per Bank of England: OK. Source: https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/quarterly-bulletin/2016/boe-notes-the-switch-to-polymer.pdf
