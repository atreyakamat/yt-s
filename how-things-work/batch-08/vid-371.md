## Fact check

- Before PTFE, cooks relied on fats and seasoned cast iron (and enamel) to reduce sticking.
- 1938: Roy Plunkett at DuPont found that a cylinder of tetrafluoroethylene gas gave no gas although it weighed as if full; cut open, it held a white waxy solid, polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE).
- PTFE is extremely non-stick, which made bonding it to metal difficult.
- Marc Grégoire, a French engineer, developed a way to fix PTFE to aluminium (patent 1954); his wife Colette is credited with suggesting cookware. Their company began selling non-stick pans in 1956.
