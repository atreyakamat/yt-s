## Fact check

- William George Horner described a cylindrical version (the Daedaleum) in January 1834; William Ensign Lincoln's 1865 version became the zoetrope, sold as a toy by Milton Bradley in 1866. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoetrope
- A spinning drum with vertical slits lets viewers see a sequence of images on the inside wall; the slits stop the pictures blurring together. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoetrope
- Name from Greek zoe (life) and tropos (turning), "wheel of life", coined by Lincoln. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoetrope
