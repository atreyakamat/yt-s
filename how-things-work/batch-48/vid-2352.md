## Fact check

- An AFCI breaks the circuit when it detects the electric arcs that are a signature of loose connections; loose connections can become hot enough to ignite house fires. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc-fault_circuit_interrupter
- Conventional breakers respond only to overloads and short circuits, so they do not protect against arcing that produces erratic, often reduced current. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc-fault_circuit_interrupter
- The electronics detect current at characteristic frequencies, usually around 100 kHz, sustained for more than a few milliseconds; it distinguishes harmless arcs (switches, plugs, brushed motors) from dangerous ones (lamp cord with a broken conductor). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc-fault_circuit_interrupter
- US NEC from 1999 required AFCIs on bedroom circuits; as of 2014 protection is required on most residential outlets; in Western Europe and the UK use is optional, mandated only in specified high-risk locations. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc-fault_circuit_interrupter
