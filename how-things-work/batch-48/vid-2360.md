## Fact check

- A callus is thickened, sometimes hardened skin formed in response to repeated friction or pressure; most often on hands and feet. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Callus
- Calluses are formed by accumulation of terminally differentiated keratinocytes (dead cells) in the outermost layer, resistant to damage thanks to cross-linked proteins and keratin filaments. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Callus
- On the feet they form at the ball of the foot, heels and small toes; (palms are noted as natural callus sites: 'palmar or plantar skin'). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Callus
- Too much friction too fast for the skin to develop a callus causes a blister or abrasion instead. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Callus
- A corn is a cone-shaped callus that penetrates into the dermis; its shape intensifies pressure at the tip. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Callus
