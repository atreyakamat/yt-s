## Fact check

- Before about 1800, nails were hand-wrought by blacksmiths or nailers individually.
- Nails were valuable enough that people burned abandoned buildings to recover them; a colonial Virginia law discouraged this by giving departing settlers nails in exchange. Year dropped (sources do not agree on it). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nail_(fastener) (Virginia law against burning houses for nails; no year given).
- Cut nails, sheared from iron plate by machine, became common from around 1800; wire nails took over in the late 1800s.
- Modern wire-nail machines produce hundreds of nails per minute.
- Cut nails from about 1800, wire nails overtaking by the late 1800s: OK, wire nails outnumbered cut nails in the US by 1892. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nail_(fastener)
