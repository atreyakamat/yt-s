## Fact check

- Medieval and early modern Europeans typically ate with knives, spoons and fingers; large two-tined forks were used for carving and serving.
- Table forks were used in the Byzantine Empire and reached Italy around the 11th century; a Byzantine bride of a Venetian doge was famously criticised by churchman Peter Damian for using a golden fork, seen as vanity.
- Thomas Coryat described forks after travelling in Italy and brought the habit to England in 1608, where he was mocked for it.
- Forks with three and then four curved tines became common in the 17th-18th centuries.
- Table forks from Byzantium to Italy: SOFTEN to Middle Ages (commonplace in Italy by the 14th century); England 1608 per Coryat. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fork
