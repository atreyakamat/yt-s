## Fact check

- Halogen lamps are tungsten filament lamps in a small fused-quartz envelope filled with gas containing a halogen (iodine or bromine).
- The halogen cycle: evaporated tungsten combines with the halogen near the hot wall instead of depositing, and the compound breaks down near the filament, redepositing tungsten (unevenly). This keeps the envelope clear.
- The cycle needs a wall temperature of roughly 250 °C or more, hence quartz.
- Manufacturers warn not to touch the quartz: skin oils cause hot spots that can weaken it and shorten its life.
