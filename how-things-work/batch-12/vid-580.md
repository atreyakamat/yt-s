## Fact check

- Pulse oximeters use red (~660 nm) and infrared (~940 nm) LEDs and a photodetector across the finger.
- Oxygenated haemoglobin absorbs more infrared and less red; deoxygenated haemoglobin absorbs more red.
- The LEDs are switched rapidly in sequence (typically hundreds of times per second) so the detector can separate the two signals.
- The device isolates the pulsatile (arterial) component to estimate oxygen saturation, and also reports pulse rate. Educational only.
