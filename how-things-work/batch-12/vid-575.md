## Fact check

- Mains frequency is 50 Hz in Britain and Europe and 60 Hz in North America; synchronous generators on a grid all turn in step with it.
- When demand exceeds generation, generators slow and frequency falls; when generation exceeds demand it rises.
- Great Britain's statutory limits are 50 Hz ± 1% (49.5-50.5 Hz).
- Grid batteries provide fast frequency response services that act within about a second.
- 'TV pickup': demand surges when millions boil kettles after popular broadcasts, and the grid operator schedules extra generation in advance.
- 50 Hz Britain, 60 Hz Americas: OK. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utility_frequency
- Half-hertz band: SOFTENED to "about half a hertz" (no source retrieved; GB statutory band is believed to be 50 Hz +/-1 percent). Source: none retrieved
