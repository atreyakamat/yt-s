## Fact check

- USB flash drives contain a USB connector, a controller chip and NAND flash memory (plus usually an LED and a crystal).
- Flash memory is non-volatile and stores data as trapped charge.
- The controller reports capacity to the host; counterfeit drives have firmware reporting a false larger capacity.
- On fake drives, writes past the real capacity wrap around or are discarded, corrupting files. Tools exist to test real capacity.
- Bar values are an illustrative example of a fake 2 TB stick with 32 GB of real memory.
