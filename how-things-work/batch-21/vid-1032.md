## Fact check

- Freeze-drying (lyophilisation): food is frozen, then placed under vacuum and gently heated so ice sublimates directly to vapour.
- Because the structure stays frozen, the food keeps its shape and becomes porous.
- Freeze-drying typically removes about 98-99% of water; fruit like strawberries starts around 90% water. Bars are approximate.
- Low water content and sealed packaging give a shelf life of years; freeze-dried food has been used on space missions and by hikers.
