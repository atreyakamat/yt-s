## Fact check

- Vapour chambers are flat heat pipes: sealed copper enclosures with a wick structure and a small amount of working fluid (usually water) under partial vacuum.
- At reduced pressure water boils well below 100 °C; around 40 °C at about 7 kPa ('about 40').
- The fluid evaporates at the heat source, vapour travels to cooler regions and condenses, releasing latent heat; the wick returns liquid by capillary action.
- Effective thermal conductivity of vapour chambers/heat pipes greatly exceeds solid copper.
