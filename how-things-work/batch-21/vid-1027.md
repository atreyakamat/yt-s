## Fact check

- Pipe (Stillson-type) wrenches have serrated jaws and a hook jaw with a small amount of play; the jaws are not parallel.
- Turning force rocks the hook jaw so the jaws pinch the pipe harder as force increases (self-tightening), and the wrench grips in one direction only.
- Reversing the swing lets the jaws loosen and slip round, giving a ratchet-like action.
- The teeth mark the surface, so pipe wrenches are not used on finished fittings or nuts.
