## Fact check

- In a turn the outer wheel follows a larger radius, so it must rotate faster; a solid axle would force one tyre to scrub/skid.
- An open differential uses a ring gear, carrier, spider (pinion) gears and side gears; going straight the spiders don't rotate on their shafts, and in turns they let the side gears turn at different speeds while their average equals the carrier speed.
- The 90/100/110 % bars are illustrative of that average relationship for a typical turn, not a measured figure.
- Open differentials send equal torque to both wheels, limited by the wheel with least grip, so a wheel on ice can spin: the reason limited-slip and locking differentials exist.
