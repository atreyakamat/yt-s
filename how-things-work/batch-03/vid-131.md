## Fact check

- 1810: Peter Durand received a British patent for preserving food in tin(-plated) containers.
- Early cans were made of heavy tin-plated wrought iron and supplied mainly to armies and navies.
- Early cans carried instructions to open them with a chisel and hammer; soldiers used bayonets, knives or rocks.
- First dedicated can openers: Robert Yeates (UK, 1855) and Ezra Warner (US, 1858), once thinner steel cans were common.
- 1870: William Lyman patented a rotating wheel cutter. 1920s: openers added a serrated gear wheel to turn the can (Star Can Opener Company, 1925).
- Tin can patent 1810 Britain, first can openers 1850s (Warner 1858), rotating wheel cutter 1870: OK. Source: https://familytreemagazine.com/history/history-of-the-can-opener
