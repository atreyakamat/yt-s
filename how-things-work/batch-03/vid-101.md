## Fact check

- Kettle noise comes mainly from steam bubbles forming at the hot element and collapsing (condensing) when they meet cooler water above; each collapse takes a tiny fraction of a second.
- As the bulk water approaches 100 °C, bubbles survive the trip to the surface instead of collapsing, so the sharp noise falls before a full rolling boil. This is a widely explained effect in physics outreach.
