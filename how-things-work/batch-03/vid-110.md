## Fact check

- Pole-mounted distribution transformers step primary voltages of a few thousand volts (7,200 V phase-to-ground is a common North American value, labelled 'typical') down to 120/240 V for homes.
- A transformer is two windings on a shared iron core; alternating current makes an alternating magnetic field, and the voltage ratio follows the ratio of turns.
- These transformers are filled with insulating mineral oil, which cools the windings and insulates them.
- A single pole transformer typically feeds a handful of homes (often under about ten).
