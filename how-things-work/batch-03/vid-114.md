## Fact check

- Typical glow sticks hold hydrogen peroxide in a thin inner glass ampoule, surrounded by a phenyl oxalate ester and fluorescent dye in the plastic outer tube.
- Bending breaks the glass so the liquids mix; the reaction produces a high-energy intermediate that transfers energy to the dye, which emits light (chemiluminescence).
- The dye determines the color.
- The reaction gives off very little heat, so it is 'cold light'.
- Reaction rate rises with temperature: warm sticks are brighter but shorter-lived, cold ones dimmer but longer-lasting.
