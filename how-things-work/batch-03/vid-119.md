## Fact check

- Open fires are inefficient; much of the heat goes up the chimney with the smoke. No percentage given.
- Warming pans (a lidded metal pan of embers on a long handle) were used to warm beds from the 1500s-1800s; lids were often pierced.
- Frost forming on the inside of single-glazed windows in unheated bedrooms is widely remembered in the UK before central heating.
- Roman hypocausts: a furnace pushed hot air through a space under raised floors (pilae) and up flues in the walls.
- The cast iron sectional radiator was developed in the 1850s (Franz San Galli, around 1855-57); central heating became common in homes during the 1900s.
- Hot water radiator invented in the 1850s (San Galli, 1855), though the claim is disputed: OK as 'invented in the 1800s'. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_San_Galli
