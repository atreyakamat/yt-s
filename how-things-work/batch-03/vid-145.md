## Fact check

- CO2 extinguishers store carbon dioxide as a liquid under its own vapour pressure, roughly 55-60 bar at room temperature; '~57 bar' is shown (about 57 bar at 20 C).
- On release, rapid expansion cools the CO2; some forms solid 'snow' at about -78 °C, the sublimation point of CO2.
- CO2 works by displacing oxygen, leaves no residue and is used on electrical fires.
- Because vapour pressure stays constant while liquid remains, a gauge is not useful; CO2 extinguishers are checked by weight. Bars show a 2 kg unit as an example.
- Users are warned not to hold the horn because it gets extremely cold.
- Discharged CO2 forms dry ice, about -78 C: OK. Source: https://www.fireengineering.com/firefighting/carbon-dioxide-fire-extinguishing-systems/
- Pressure at room temperature: FIXED from ~50 to ~57 bar (about 58.6 bar at 21 C). Source: https://www.fireengineering.com/firefighting/carbon-dioxide-fire-extinguishing-systems/
