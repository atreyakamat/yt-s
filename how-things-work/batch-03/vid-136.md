## Fact check

- Lithium-ion cells have a graphite anode, a lithium metal oxide cathode, a liquid electrolyte, and a porous polymer separator; layers are wound (jelly roll) or stacked/folded.
- On charge, lithium ions move from the cathode through the electrolyte and intercalate into graphite; on discharge they return while electrons flow through the external circuit.
- Separators are microporous films (often around 10-25 micrometres thick, thinner than paper at about 70-100 micrometres) that let ions pass while keeping electrodes apart.
