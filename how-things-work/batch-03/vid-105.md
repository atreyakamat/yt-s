## Fact check

- Saponification: fats or oils react with a strong alkali (sodium hydroxide, 'lye') to make soap and glycerol. True soap bars are made this way; some 'beauty bars' are synthetic detergents instead.
- Soap molecules are surfactants: a water-loving (hydrophilic) carboxylate head and a grease-loving (hydrophobic) hydrocarbon tail.
- Surfactants surround oil droplets in structures called micelles, which water can carry away.
