## Fact check

- Early wired TV remotes appeared around 1950 (Zenith's 'Lazy Bones'), connected to the set by a cable.
- In 1955 a light-beam remote (Flash-Matic) used photocells in the TV; sunlight could trigger them.
- In 1956 the 'Space Command' remote used no batteries: buttons struck aluminium rods that rang at ultrasonic frequencies detected by the TV; the click gave remotes the nickname 'clicker'.
- Infrared remotes replaced ultrasonic ones later; the exact switch date varies by source, so the script just says 'later'.
- Wired remote about 1950, light-beam Flash-Matic 1955, ultrasound Space Command 1956: OK. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_control
- Infrared 'took over in the 1980s': SOFTENED to 'later' as sources give no clean date. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_control
