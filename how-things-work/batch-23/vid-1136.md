## Fact check

- Pepper mills use a conical burr (grooved cone) turning inside a stationary toothed ring, driven by a central shaft from the top.
- The spiral grooves feed peppercorns into the tapering gap; a top nut or knob adjusts cone position and so grind size.
- Volatile aroma compounds in pepper dissipate after grinding, which is why fresh-ground pepper is more aromatic.
- Salt is corrosive to ordinary steel, so salt mills commonly use ceramic (or stainless/nylon) mechanisms.
