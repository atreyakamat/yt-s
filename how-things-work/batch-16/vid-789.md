## Fact check

- Feather shuttlecocks have 16 overlapping goose (or duck) feathers fixed into a rounded cork base covered with thin leather.
- The heavy nose and high-drag skirt put the center of pressure behind the center of mass, so the shuttle turns to fly cork-first.
- Elite smashes exceed 300 km/h off the racket (records are over 500 km/h).
- The skirt's high drag decelerates the shuttle rapidly, so it arrives far slower than it left.
- Quality shuttles use feathers from the same wing so their natural curve makes the shuttle rotate consistently.
- 16 feathers, cork base, turns to fly cork first, feathered shuttles leave strings at over 565 km/h: OK. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuttlecock
