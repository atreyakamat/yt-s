## Fact check

- The wagon-wheel effect is an illusion where a spoked wheel appears to rotate differently from its true rotation: slower, stationary or backwards; in recorded media it results from temporal aliasing. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagon-wheel_effect
- Spoke example: if the next spoke lands at 11:30 the wheel looks like it rotates backwards; if the spoke that was at 9 moves to 12 it looks stationary. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagon-wheel_effect
- Gas discharge lamps (fluorescent, neon etc.) flicker at twice the power line frequency, e.g. 100 times per second on a 50-cycle line. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagon-wheel_effect
- Turntable platters have markings calibrated so that under local mains frequency (50 or 60 Hz) they appear static at exactly 33 1/3 rpm. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagon-wheel_effect
- Rushton (1967) observed the effect under continuous illumination while humming, which vibrates the eyes; humming at a multiple of the rotation frequency stopped the rotation. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagon-wheel_effect
