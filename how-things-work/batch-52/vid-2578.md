## Fact check

- A mortar is a bowl (hardwood, metal, ceramic or hard stone) and a pestle is a blunt club-shaped object; the food is pounded, pressed or rotated against it. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortar_and_pestle
- Crushing uses little energy so the substance does not warm up. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortar_and_pestle
- Rough ceramic grinds very fine powders; smooth non-porous materials are preferred so they do not absorb what is ground; wood is highly absorbent. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortar_and_pestle
- Molcajete: basalt version used by pre-Hispanic Mesoamerican cultures including the Aztec and Maya, still common in Mexican cooking, needs seasoning before use. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortar_and_pestle
- Mortars and pestles from Southwest Asia date to about 35000 BC; Raqefet Cave mortars around 10000 BC used by Late Natufians to grind cereals for beer; Wedgwood mortar 1759. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortar_and_pestle
