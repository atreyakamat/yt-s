## Fact check

- Modern lume (e.g. strontium aluminate doped with europium) is phosphorescent: absorbed light excites electrons into trap states that release light slowly.
- Afterglow is brightest just after charging and decays over hours.
- Early 20th-century watches used radium-based luminous paint, which glowed without charging but was radioactive; it was phased out by around the 1960s.
- Some watches today use sealed tritium gas tubes instead; the script doesn't claim all use phosphorescent paint.
