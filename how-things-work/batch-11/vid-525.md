## Fact check

- Masking tape was invented in 1925 by Richard Drew, a lab assistant who saw auto-body painters struggling with two-tone paint jobs; existing tapes and glues pulled off the fresh paint.
- It uses a creped (crinkled) paper backing that stretches and conforms to curves and tears by hand, with a low-tack adhesive designed for clean removal.
- Heat and UV can cure the adhesive and leave residue if tape is left on too long; makers rate tapes for limited days of exposure.
