## Fact check

- Most handheld stage mics are cardioid or similar directional types: rear ports in the head let sound from behind partly cancel, so they favor the front.
- Cupping the grille blocks those ports, making the mic closer to omnidirectional.
- That increases pickup of loudspeaker and monitor sound, making acoustic feedback (a howl or squeal) more likely.
- Cupping also changes the tone, typically making it muffled and bass-heavy.
- Standard live-sound advice is to hold the mic by the handle.
