## Fact check

- Driving a screw displaces wood fibres; near ends and edges, and in hardwoods, the wedging can split the wood.
- Standard guidance: drill a pilot hole about the diameter of the screw's root (core) so threads still cut into the surrounding wood; a hole that is too large gives poor grip.
- The common visual check: hold the bit in front of the screw; the core should be hidden while the threads remain visible.
- A clearance hole in the top piece (at least the shank diameter) lets the screw clamp the two pieces together instead of jacking them apart.
