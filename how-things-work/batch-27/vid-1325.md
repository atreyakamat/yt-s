## Fact check

- The Thames Tunnel (1825-1843), by Marc Brunel with his son Isambard, is regarded as the first tunnel successfully built under a navigable river.
- Brunel's tunnelling shield (patented 1818) was inspired by the shipworm, which bores into timber protected by its shell.
- The river broke in several times; after a flood in 1828 work stopped until 1835. The tunnel opened in 1843, 18 years after work began.
- It became a railway tunnel in 1869 and is used by London Overground trains today; modern tunnel boring machines descend from the shield idea.
- Thames Tunnel opened 25 March 1843, first tunnel built under a navigable river, flooded several times (1827, 1828). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thames_Tunnel
- Shipworm inspiration for Brunel's shield (patented 1818) is 'claimed', so wording softened to 'reportedly'. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Isambard_Brunel
