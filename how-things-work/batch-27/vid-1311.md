## Fact check

- Snap-off utility blades are scored with diagonal lines so segments can be broken off to expose a fresh edge.
- The design was invented in 1956 by Yoshio Okada in Japan, reportedly inspired by shards of broken glass and segmented chocolate bars.
- Common 18 mm blades have about 13 segments (varies by size).
- Snap-off blade: OLFA, Japan, 1956, inspired by broken glass and chocolate bars. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utility_knife
