## Fact check

- The Edison screw (ES) base dates from the early 1880s; the screw shell is one contact and the insulated foot (tip) contact is the other, separated by glass insulation.
- Wiring standards connect the tip to live/line and the shell to neutral so the more exposed shell is the lower-risk conductor.
- E27 and E14 designate the nominal thread diameter in millimetres. Bayonet caps (e.g. B22) are also common in the UK.
- Edison screw patented 1881, bases from the 1880s; E27 = 27 mm thread diameter, E14 = small Edison screw: OK. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edison_screw
