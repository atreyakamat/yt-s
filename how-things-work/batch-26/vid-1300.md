## Fact check

- Ultrasound probes use piezoelectric elements to emit and receive sound at roughly 2-15 MHz, far above human hearing (about 20 kHz).
- Scanners assume sound travels at about 1540 m/s in soft tissue; at that speed, echoes from a few centimetres return within tens of microseconds.
- Echo delay gives depth; echo strength depends on how different the tissues are. Bars show relative depth, illustrative.
- Frames update many times per second, giving real-time images. Coupling gel removes air, which reflects nearly all ultrasound.
- 1540 m/s is the standard assumed speed of sound in soft tissue for ultrasound scanners (about 1.5 km/s): OK. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_ultrasound
