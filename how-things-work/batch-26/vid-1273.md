## Fact check

- Composition → conventional nail polish is a film former (nitrocellulose), resins, plasticisers and pigments dissolved in solvents such as ethyl and butyl acetate.
- Dries by evaporation → no chemical curing; the solvent smell is the evaporating acetates.
- Nitrocellulose film stock → early cinema film was nitrate (nitrocellulose) based.
- Thick coats → the surface dries first and traps solvent beneath, staying soft longer.
- Mixing ball → a metal ball that agitates settled pigment when shaken.
- Gel polish → cures by UV/LED-initiated polymerisation.
