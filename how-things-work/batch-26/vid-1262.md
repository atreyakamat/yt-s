## Fact check

- Earlier keyboards placed cursor keys in a row, cross or diamond layouts.
- The inverted-T cursor layout appeared on DEC's LK201 (1982) and became standard after IBM's Model M (mid-1980s).
- It allows three fingers to rest on left/down/right with the middle finger reaching up, and it is isolated by gaps for touch location.
- WASD in games mirrors the inverted-T layout.
- Inverted-T arrow keys established by the DEC LK201 in 1982 and then became the industry standard; layouts varied in the 1970s: OK. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrow_keys
