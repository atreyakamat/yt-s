## Fact check

- A fishing hook has an eye, shank, bend and point; most have a barb, a small backward-facing projection behind the point.
- The barb lets the point penetrate but resists the hook backing out.
- Barbless hooks are easier and quicker to remove, which is why they're favored for catch-and-release.
- Some fisheries regulations require barbless hooks; anglers often flatten barbs with pliers.
