## Fact check

- Drywall screws have a bugle head: a curved underside that compresses the gypsum board's face paper rather than cutting it like a sharp countersunk head.
- The face paper provides much of the board's tensile strength at fasteners; torn paper reduces pull-through resistance.
- Standard practice is to set screws slightly below the surface (a 'dimple') without breaking the paper.
- They commonly have a black phosphate coating, sharp points, coarse threads for wood studs and fine threads for light steel studs.
