## Fact check

- Chopsticks are held in the dominant hand and wielded as extensions of the hand; smoothed and frequently tapered. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chopsticks
- Eating chopsticks about 23-26 cm; very long ones about 30-40 cm are used for cooking, especially deep frying (bars show rounded midpoints, labelled about). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chopsticks
- Chinese chopsticks more commonly blunt; Japanese ones tend to be sharp and pointed, to dissect fish and seafood; many Japanese chopsticks have circumferential grooves at the eating end; traditionally wood or bamboo, lacquered. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chopsticks
- Earliest evidence: six bronze chopsticks, 26 cm, from Anyang dated roughly 1200 BC, supposed to have been used for cooking. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chopsticks
- Used as eating utensils from the Han dynasty; exclusive use for serving and eating from the Ming dynasty, when they acquired the present shape; used for over three millennia. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chopsticks
