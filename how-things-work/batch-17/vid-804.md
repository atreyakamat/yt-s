## Fact check

- Common filter straws use hollow-fiber membranes: bundles of small hollow tubes whose walls have pores around 0.1-0.2 microns (0.0001-0.0002 mm).
- Water is drawn through the fiber walls; particles larger than the pores, including bacteria (typically ~0.5-5 µm) and protozoan cysts (several µm or more), are trapped.
- Hollow-fiber filters of this pore size do not reliably remove most viruses, and do not remove dissolved chemicals or salt.
- Many designs can be backflushed to clear clogged fibers.
- This describes how the filter works; it is not health advice.
