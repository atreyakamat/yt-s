## Fact check

- Aerial marker spheres make overhead wires visible to aircraft; US FAA guidance specifies 20-inch or 36-inch (about 0.9 m) spheres in alternating aviation orange, white and yellow.
- They are placed where lines cross valleys, rivers or highways, or are near airports and heliports.
- Wire strikes are a known hazard for low-flying helicopters and light aircraft.
- The spheres are hollow, lightweight shells clamped to the wire, usually the top shield wire.
