## Fact check

- A compass aligns with the local magnetic field, pointing toward magnetic north, which is hundreds of kilometers from the geographic North Pole.
- Magnetic declination is the angle between true north and magnetic north; it varies with location.
- Declination is near zero along the agonic line and exceeds 15 degrees in many regions (e.g. parts of the US Pacific Northwest and New England), and is much larger in the high Arctic.
- Topographic maps print the declination (with a date) in the margin; many compasses have an adjustable declination setting.
- The magnetic pole drifts, so declination changes over years.
- Compass error of 15 degrees over 1 km is about 268 m sideways (tan 15 degrees x 1,000 m): OK. Source: https://earthsci.org/education/fieldsk/declin.html
