## Fact check

- Baseplate and lensatic hiking compasses commonly use a liquid-filled capsule with a magnetized needle on a pivot.
- Earth's magnetic field acts like a large, weak bar magnet; a free magnetized needle aligns with it.
- The liquid damps the needle's oscillation so it settles quickly instead of swinging.
- The fill is typically a light oil or similar low-freezing-point liquid so it works in cold weather.
- Bubbles can form in cold or at altitude as the liquid contracts; small ones often shrink or vanish when warm.
- Nearby magnets, steel and electronics can deflect the needle.
