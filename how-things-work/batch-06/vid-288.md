## Fact check

- Three-way catalytic converters use a ceramic (or metal) honeycomb substrate with a washcoat carrying platinum, palladium and rhodium.
- Reactions: CO is oxidised to CO2, unburnt hydrocarbons to CO2 and water, and NOx reduced to N2.
- Catalysts need to reach 'light-off' temperature, typically around 250-400 °C, so emissions are highest just after a cold start.
- Catalytic converter theft for their precious metals is widely reported by police in many countries.
- Catalytic converters use platinum, palladium and rhodium and need about 400 C to work effectively (so 'a few hundred degrees'): OK. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalytic_converter
