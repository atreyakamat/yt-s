## Fact check

- Curtain billows in with hot or cold water → so buoyant hot air (chimney effect) can't be the only cause.
- 2001 → David Schmidt (University of Massachusetts Amherst) used computational fluid dynamics to simulate a shower and found a sideways vortex with a low-pressure centre (Ig Nobel Prize, 2001).
- Pressure difference → higher pressure outside the curtain pushes it toward the low-pressure region.
- Weighted hems and magnets → common fixes that hold the curtain's bottom in place.
