## Fact check

- Fridge door gaskets contain a flexible magnetic strip (ferrite powder bonded in a flexible plastic/rubber) that pulls the gasket against the steel cabinet.
- Older fridges used mechanical latches; children trapped in discarded fridges led to the US Refrigerator Safety Act of 1956, requiring doors that can be opened from inside. Magnetic seals became the standard solution.
- A good seal reduces warm air infiltration and so energy use. The paper (or banknote) test is a common seal check recommended by appliance makers and energy advisers.
- US Refrigerator Safety Act signed 1956 (effective 1958) requires doors that open from inside, which drove magnetic gaskets: OK. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refrigerator_death
