## Fact check

- Hand sanding without a backing block concentrates pressure under the fingertips and produces uneven surfaces; a flat sanding block distributes pressure (standard woodworking advice).
- Sanding along the grain avoids cross-grain scratches, which are highlighted by stains and finishes.
- Progressing through grits (e.g. 80, 120, 180) without big jumps is standard practice; each finer grit removes the previous grit's scratches, and skipping grits makes this slow.
- Removing dust between grits stops coarse particles scratching the surface.
