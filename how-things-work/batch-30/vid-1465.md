## Fact check

- Plaster of Paris is calcium sulphate hemihydrate, made by heating gypsum (calcium sulphate dihydrate) to drive off most of its water.
- Mixing it with water re-forms interlocking gypsum crystals in an exothermic reaction, so casts feel warm while setting.
- Plaster sets in minutes but takes a day or more to reach full strength; fibreglass casts are lighter and now widely used.
- Dutch military surgeon Antonius Mathijsen introduced plaster-impregnated bandages in 1851-52.
