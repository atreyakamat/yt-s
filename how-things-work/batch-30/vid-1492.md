## Fact check

- One-component polyurethane expanding foam cans contain an isocyanate-based prepolymer and a liquefied gas propellant.
- On release the propellant vaporises, frothing the mix; the prepolymer then cures by reacting with atmospheric moisture, releasing carbon dioxide that further expands the foam.
- Manufacturers recommend lightly misting surfaces with water to aid curing, especially in dry conditions.
- Cured foam is a rigid cellular material that seals gaps and draughts; it continues to expand during curing, so gaps are part-filled, and low-expansion foam is used around window and door frames to avoid bowing.
