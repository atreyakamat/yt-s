## Fact check

- Cellulose fibres are fairly transparent; paper's opacity comes from light scattering at fibre-air interfaces.
- Tracing paper is made by heavily refining (beating) fibres so they bond densely with few air voids (some types are also chemically treated).
- Filling voids with oil or water reduces scattering, which is why greasy or wet paper becomes translucent.
- Gauge is illustrative.
