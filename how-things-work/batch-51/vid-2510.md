## Fact check

- Aristotle linked tickling to surprise; Darwin observed that the ticklee must not know exactly where the touch will land. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tickling
- Hall and Allin (1897) split tickling into knismesis (light, itchy) and gargalesis (harder, laughter-provoking); knismesis does not depend on surprise so people can induce it on themselves. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tickling
- Blakemore and colleagues (1998) used MRI to show the cerebellum predicts the sensation of self-directed touch, and a cortical mechanism suppresses the response. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tickling
- In a study of 84 college students, 32% enjoyed being tickled, 32% were neutral, 36% did not enjoy it. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tickling
