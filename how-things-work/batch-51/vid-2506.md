## Fact check

- A mortise lock requires a pocket (the mortise) cut into the edge of the door; the term describes only the method of fitting, not the key mechanism; parts: lock body, trim, strike plate. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortise_lock
- Warded locks were used in Europe from the Roman period onwards; Jeremiah Chubb's lever tumbler lock was patented in 1818; in lever locks the key moves levers that allow the bolt to move. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortise_lock
- Linus Yale Jr.'s pin tumbler mortise cylinder lock came in 1865. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortise_lock
- Mortise locks may include a non-locking latch operated by a door handle; a lock without a latch is a dead lock. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortise_lock
- Until the mid-nineteenth century mortise locks were used only in the most formal rooms of the most expensive houses; in 1805 Jefferson ordered 26 mortise locks for the principal rooms of Monticello. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortise_lock
