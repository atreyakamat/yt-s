## Fact check

- A radio clock is a quartz clock automatically synchronised to a time code transmitted by a radio transmitter connected to a time standard such as an atomic clock; it keeps time with an oscillator if the signal is momentarily unavailable. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_clock
- WWVB is a longwave time station near Fort Collins, Colorado, on a 60 kHz carrier; the time code is sent at one bit per second, a frame lasts one minute and conveys year, day, hour and minute; broadcast in UTC with clocks applying time zone and DST offsets. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WWVB
- Terrestrial time signal radio clocks usually achieve accuracy within a hundredth of a second. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_clock
- Heathkit offered one of the first radio clocks in late 1983; by 1990 Junghans miniaturised it into a digital wristwatch. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_clock
