## Fact check

- Most bird strikes hit the front of the aircraft; strikes into the engine are typically more severe. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_strike
- Most large commercial jet engines must be able to shut down safely after ingesting a bird up to 1.8 kg (4 lb); the engine does not have to survive. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_strike
- Jet aircraft structures must withstand one 4 lb (1.8 kg) bird impact anywhere, including the flight deck windshields, and one 8 lb (3.6 kg) bird on the empennage (tail). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_strike
- Testing first fired a bird carcass from a gas cannon; replaced by blocks of suitable density, often gelatin; now limited testing supported by computer simulation. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_strike
- Avian radar can track thousands of birds, updating position, speed, heading and size every 2-3 seconds; used by air traffic controllers. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_strike
- Most strikes occur below 3,000 feet (910 m); pilots should climb above 3,000 ft quickly and slow down when confronted with birds. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_strike
