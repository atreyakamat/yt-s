# vid-1615 · How a vitamin D gap shows up in winter

## Hook

- **Chosen (0-3s):** "Winter sun may not make enough vitamin D."
- Backup 1: "Why winter leaves you short on D."
- Backup 2: "Sunlight is a vitamin source."

## Fact check (verify before posting)

Above roughly 37°N/S (e.g. much of Europe/US north) winter sun produces little to no cutaneous vitamin D; deficiency is common in winter; doses should be guided by clinicians. Don't give dose numbers.

## Voiceover (read-through)

1. Winter sun may not make enough vitamin D.
2. Your skin makes vitamin D from UVB sunlight.
3. At higher latitudes winter UVB is too weak.
4. Food gives some, like oily fish and eggs.
5. Many people run low in winter.
6. A blood test and a doctor can advise on supplements.
