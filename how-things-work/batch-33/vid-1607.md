# vid-1607 · Why learning a skill takes more than 10,000 hours

## Hook

- **Chosen (0-3s):** "The ten thousand hour rule is oversimplified."
- Backup 1: "The most misquoted number in learning."
- Backup 2: "Deliberate practice beats raw hours."

## Fact check (verify before posting)

Ericsson et al. (1993) reported elite violin students averaged ~10,000 h by age 20 (best group); popularised by Gladwell; Ericsson said deliberate practice quality, not a magic number. Meta-analyses show practice explains a portion of skill variance. Verified: 10,000 hours popularised by Gladwell; critics say practice explains limited variance: OK. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outliers_(book)

## Voiceover (read-through)

1. The ten thousand hour rule is oversimplified.
2. It came from a study of elite violin students.
3. Practice time did matter a lot for them.
4. But the number 10,000 was an average, not a rule.
5. Quality, feedback and field all change the hours.
6. You can get good far sooner than elite.
