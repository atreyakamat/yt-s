# vid-1647 · How coffee beans become coffee

## Hook

## Fact check (verify before posting)

Coffee 'beans' are seeds of Coffea fruit (cherries). Roasting triggers Maillard reactions; over 800 volatile compounds identified in roasted coffee (commonly cited 800-1000). Fine. Verified: Over 850 aroma compounds after roasting: OK for 800+. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_chemical_compounds_in_coffee

## Voiceover (read-through)

1. Coffee beans are really seeds.
2. They sit inside red fruit called cherries.
3. The seeds are dried, then roasted.
4. Roasting turns green seeds brown and brings out flavour.
5. Hundreds of compounds form during roasting.
6. That's why fresh-ground coffee smells so strong.
