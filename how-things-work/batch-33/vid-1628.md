## Fact check (verify before posting)

Polar bear guard hairs are unpigmented and translucent/hollow-ish (the hollow core claim is simplified); white appearance arises from light scattering; skin is black. Note: the 'fibre-optic heat trapping' idea was debunked; insulation is mostly fur and blubber.

## Voiceover (read-through)

1. A polar bear's fur isn't actually white.
2. Each hair is a clear hollow tube.
3. Light scatters inside and bounces back as white.
4. Underneath, the skin is black.
5. Black skin absorbs the warmth of sunlight.
6. Fat and thick fur do most of the insulating.
