# vid-2836 · Why cooking oil has a smoke point

## Fact check (verify before posting)

Smoke point is the temperature at which an oil visibly smokes and degrades; it is generally higher for refined oils than unrefined. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoke_point

## Voiceover (read-through)

1. Every oil has a temperature where it smokes.
2. Past a point, oil starts to break down.
3. It releases visible smoke and bitter compounds.
4. Refined oils tend to tolerate more heat.
5. Pick the oil for the job, not just the flavour.
