# vid-2801 · Why a water droplet dances on a hot pan

## Hook

- **Chosen (0-3s):** "Water can float on a hot pan."
- Backup 1: "Water that refuses to touch the pan."
- Backup 2: "The vapour cushion under a drop."

## Fact check (verify before posting)

Leidenfrost effect: above the Leidenfrost point (roughly 200 C for water on a smooth pan, varies by surface) a vapour layer insulates the drop. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leidenfrost_effect

## Voiceover (read-through)

1. Water can float on a hot pan.
2. Above a certain heat, the bottom of the drop turns to steam instantly.
3. That steam forms a cushion between water and metal.
4. With no contact, heat moves in slowly, so the drop lasts.
5. Cooler pans boil the water away fast, with no dancing.
