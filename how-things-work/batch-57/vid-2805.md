# vid-2805 · Why bread crust turns brown

## Hook

- **Chosen (0-3s):** "Brown crust is chemistry, not just burning."
- Backup 1: "The reaction that makes crust taste good."
- Backup 2: "Browning isn't burning."

## Fact check (verify before posting)

The Maillard reaction typically proceeds noticeably above about 140 C (284 F) and needs low surface moisture. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maillard_reaction

## Voiceover (read-through)

1. Brown crust is chemistry, not just burning.
2. It's the Maillard reaction between sugars and amino acids.
3. It starts around 140 degrees Celsius on the surface.
4. Hundreds of new flavour and aroma molecules form.
5. Burning is different: that's carbon, and it tastes bitter.
