## Fact check

- Roman tersorium → a sponge on a stick found in Roman latrine contexts; most historians think it was used for wiping and rinsed in a water channel or vinegar (some debate, hence 'likely').
- Greek stones and pottery (pessoi) → archaeological and literary evidence for wiping with stones and potsherds.
- Leaves, moss, hay, snow, water → widely documented historical alternatives.
- China → Yan Zhitui mentioned paper for wiping in 589 AD; in 1393 the Ming court ordered large sheets for imperial use.
- 1857 → Joseph Gayetty's packaged flat toilet paper; rolls spread in the late 1800s.
- Corncobs and catalogue pages → common in rural 1800s America.
- Romans used a sponge on a stick (tersorium); Greeks used stones/pottery: OK. China used paper for wiping by the 6th century (500s): OK. 1393 imperial court paper: only one source (National Geographic) says rice-based toilet paper was mass-produced for the imperial family by 1393. Gayetty 1857 packaged paper: OK. Source: https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/what-people-do-before-toilet-paper
