## Fact check

- The speed of sound in air at 15 °C at sea level is about 343 m/s, roughly 1,235 km/h.
- An aircraft creates pressure waves; above Mach 1 they merge into shock waves forming a cone (Mach cone).
- The boom is the sudden pressure change as the shock passes an observer; it lasts a fraction of a second.
- The boom is heard along a 'boom carpet' under the whole supersonic flight path, not just where the plane passes Mach 1.
- Supersonic civil flight over land is banned in many countries (e.g. the US); Concorde flew supersonic mainly over oceans.
