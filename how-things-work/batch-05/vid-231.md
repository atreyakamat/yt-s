## Fact check

- Paper is made from cellulose fibres pulped from wood (or recycled paper) by mechanical or chemical cooking.
- Pulp at the headbox of a paper machine is typically around 99% water (well under 1% fibre).
- Paper strength comes mainly from hydrogen bonding between cellulose fibres formed as the sheet dries; sizing additives improve surface properties but the sheet holds without glue.
- Water disrupts fibre hydrogen bonds, which is why untreated paper weakens when wet.
- Pulp stock leaves the headbox at about 0.5% fibre, so about 99.5% water: OK for 'about 99 percent'. Source: https://patents.google.com/patent/US6702925
