## Fact check

- Two-piece drink cans are made by the draw-and-wall-iron process: a disc is blanked from aluminium coil, drawn into a cup, then forced through ironing rings that thin and lengthen the wall.
- Can walls are roughly 0.1 mm thick; the base is thicker and is domed.
- The body has no seam; the lid (end) is made separately from thicker, stronger alloy.
- Bodies are trimmed, decorated, internally coated and necked so a smaller-diameter lid fits.
- A single bodymaker produces a few hundred cans per minute, so 'hundreds a minute' is conservative.
- Drawn-and-ironed aluminium can side wall is about 0.08-0.12 mm: OK for 'about 0.1 mm'. Body is one seamless piece, lid made separately: OK. Source: https://patents.google.com/patent/EP0118926A2
