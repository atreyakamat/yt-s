## Fact check

- Galileo is credited with an air thermoscope around 1593; it had no standard scale.
- Daniel Gabriel Fahrenheit made a mercury-in-glass thermometer in 1714 and proposed his scale in 1724.
- Early clinical thermometers were about a foot long and took around 20 minutes to read; Thomas Clifford Allbutt introduced a roughly six-inch clinical thermometer in 1867 that took about five minutes.
- Electronic digital thermometers became common in the later 20th century; '1980s' is an approximate date.
- Allbutt's 1867 pocket clinical thermometer: about six inches long, read in about five minutes, versus about a foot long and about twenty minutes before: OK. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_Allbutt
- Galileo thermoscope about 1593 and Fahrenheit mercury thermometer 1714: standard dates, not re-searched this pass.
