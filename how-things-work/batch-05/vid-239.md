## Fact check

- 'Window' comes from Old Norse 'vindauga', literally 'wind eye'.
- Early openings were unglazed holes with shutters; oiled linen, parchment and thin horn were used before glass became affordable.
- Window glass was costly in medieval and Tudor England; it was mostly found in churches and wealthy homes, and glazed casements were sometimes treated as movable property and taken when moving.
- England's window tax ran from 1696 to 1851; many windows were bricked up to avoid it, and some bricked-up windows survive.
