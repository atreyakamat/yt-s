## Fact check

- Pneumatic nailers typically run at roughly 70-120 psi; 100 psi is about 7 times atmospheric pressure (14.7 psi), so 'several times' holds.
- Pulling the trigger opens a valve that releases stored air (the handle acts as a reservoir) onto a piston carrying a driver blade, which drives the nail in one stroke.
- The piston is returned (by air, often via a return chamber, or a bumper/spring) and the magazine feeds the next nail.
- A drive cycle takes a few tens of milliseconds; a blink takes roughly 100-400 ms.
- Nailers have a contact (safety) nose that must be depressed against the work before firing.
