## Fact check

- Pocket watches on chains dominated men's timekeeping from the 1600s until the early 1900s.
- Early wristwatches (e.g. one made in 1810 for Caroline Murat, Queen of Naples) were treated as women's jewellery.
- Soldiers in late-1800s campaigns and especially the First World War adopted 'trench watches', often with radium luminous dials.
- After the war, wristwatches became normal for men and overtook pocket watches through the 1920s-30s.
- Breguet made a wrist-worn watch for Caroline Murat, Queen of Naples, commissioned 1810 (delivered 1812; the watch is lost): OK as 'early wristwatch for a queen' 1810. Source: https://www.breguet.com/en/timeline/1801-1823/milestones/first-wristwatch-7579
