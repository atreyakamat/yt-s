## Fact check

- Candle combustion: the flame melts wax, capillary action draws liquid wax up the wick, heat vaporises it, and the vapour burns (as Faraday described in 'The Chemical History of a Candle', 1848).
- The yellow luminous zone comes from incandescent soot (carbon) particles; the blue base is where combustion is more complete; the dark inner zone is unburnt vapour.
- Braided wicks (introduced in the 1820s) curl as they burn so the tip reaches the hot outer flame and burns away; older twisted wicks needed regular snuffing/trimming.
