## Fact check

- Slotted screws were the norm for centuries; screws became machine-made in quantity during the 1800s.
- The cross-head design was developed by John P. Thompson and taken up by Henry F. Phillips's company, with patents in the mid-1930s; General Motors (Cadillac) adopted it on assembly lines in 1936.
- Its tapered recess self-centres the driver, which suited powered drivers on assembly lines.
- The tapered walls cause cam-out under high torque (whether this was intended is debated, so the script doesn't claim intent).
- Phillips: John P. Thompson patented the cross recess in 1932; Henry Phillips bought it (about 1935), patents 1936, GM used it on 1936/37 Cadillacs: OK for 'in the 1930s' and '1936 assembly lines adopt it' (some sources say 1937). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_F._Phillips
