## Fact check

- Faults (lightning, trees touching lines) cause currents many times normal; protective relays detect them within about a cycle and trip breakers.
- High-voltage breakers must extinguish the arc between parting contacts; SF6 'puffer' breakers blast gas across the arc until it goes out at a current zero.
- Transmission faults are typically cleared in roughly 3-5 cycles (about 60-100 ms at 50 Hz); a blink takes roughly 100-400 ms, so the bar values are representative.
- Distribution lines use auto-reclosers that re-energise after a short pause, which is why lights flicker off and back on; if the fault persists, they trip again.
- Grid breaker clearing time under 0.1 s: not independently sourced this pass (typical clearing is a few cycles, about 40-60 ms).
