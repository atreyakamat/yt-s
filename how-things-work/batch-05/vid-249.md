## Fact check

- The best-known kids' modeling dough began as a wallpaper cleaning compound made by a Cincinnati soap company in the 1930s, used to lift coal soot from non-washable wallpaper.
- Demand fell as homes moved from coal to oil and gas heating and washable vinyl wallpaper appeared after WWII.
- In the 1950s a nursery school teacher (Kay Zufall) used it for kids' crafts, and the company relaunched it as a toy around 1956 with colors and a scent.
- Its publicly known main ingredients are flour, water and salt (the full recipe is a trade secret).
- No brand name is used, per channel rules.
- Play-Doh began as a wallpaper cleaner in the 1930s (Kutol, Cincinnati) and became a toy in the mid-1950s after a teacher (Kay Zufall) suggested it for classroom crafts: OK for 'in the 1950s'. Source: https://www.museumofplay.org/blog/the-history-of-play-doh-good-clean-fun/
