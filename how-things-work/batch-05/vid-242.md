## Fact check

- Dial-up modems modulated digital data into audio tones over the phone network; mass home use in the 1990s.
- The connection noise is the handshake: the modems probe the line and negotiate a speed and protocol.
- Dial-up tied up the phone line unless you had a second line.
- 56 kbit/s was the top speed (V.90/V.92); a 3-4 MB song at real-world 40-50 kbit/s takes around 10 minutes.
- 1 Gbit/s ÷ 56 kbit/s ≈ 17,900, so 'nearly eighteen thousand times'.
- 56k modem top speed 56 kbit/s; a 3-5 MB song takes about 10 minutes at real-world 56k speeds (about 6.6 KB/s): OK as 'around ten minutes'. Source: https://www.bgr.com/2294829/how-long-to-download-100-mb-56k-modem/
- 1 Gbit/s / 56 kbit/s is about 17,900: arithmetic, OK as 'nearly eighteen thousand'.
