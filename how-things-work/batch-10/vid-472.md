## Fact check

- Standard caulking guns use a friction drive: the trigger tilts a drive (grip) plate whose hole binds on the smooth plunger rod and pushes it forward; a spring returns the plate.
- A second locking plate at the rear, held tilted by a spring, stops the rod moving backward; pressing its thumb tab releases it.
- Pressure stays on the cartridge after squeezing, so caulk keeps flowing unless the rod is released (or the gun has a 'dripless' auto-release).
