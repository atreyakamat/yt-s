## Fact check

- The 'featherie' golf ball was used from around the early 1600s until the mid-1800s: a stitched leather pouch stuffed with boiled goose or chicken feathers.
- A top hat full of feathers per ball is the traditional description; as the ball dried, the leather shrank and the feathers expanded, making it hard.
- Makers could only produce a few per day, so featheries were expensive.
- The gutta-percha 'guttie', molded from the dried sap of the gutta-percha tree, appeared around 1848 and was far cheaper.
- Coburn Haskell's wound rubber-core ball was patented in 1898 and flew farther, replacing the guttie in the early 1900s.
- Featherie era from about 1618, gutta-percha ball 1848, Haskell wound-rubber ball patented 1899: OK. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golf_ball
