## Fact check

- Weight-valve (jiggler/whistle) pressure cookers have a weighted cap sitting on a vent pipe; when steam force on the vent area exceeds the weight, the cap lifts and steam escapes with a whistle, then the cap reseats.
- Pressure = weight / vent area, so the weight sets the operating pressure, about 15 psi (1 bar) above atmospheric on many cookers, where water boils at about 120 °C.
- Counting whistles is a common cooking-time method, especially in Indian home cooking.
