## Fact check

- Dried shark skin (and ray skin) was used as an abrasive by woodworkers; its surface is covered with tooth-like dermal denticles.
- Pumice and horsetail (scouring rush, rich in silica) were traditional smoothing and polishing materials.
- The earliest recorded sandpaper is from 13th-century China: crushed shells, seeds and sand bonded to parchment with natural gum.
- Glass paper and sandpaper were manufactured in the 1800s; waterproof abrasive papers came in the 1900s.
- For coarse and medium grits, the grit number corresponds roughly to the number of openings per linear inch in the sieve used to grade the particles, so higher numbers are finer.
- Earliest coated abrasive like sandpaper: 13th-century China, crushed shells, seeds and sand on parchment with gum: OK. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandpaper
