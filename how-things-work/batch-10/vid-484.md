## Fact check

- Karl Drais of Baden (Germany) introduced his Laufmaschine ('running machine', also called draisine or dandy horse) in 1817.
- It had two in-line wheels and a steerable front wheel; riders pushed along with their feet.
- It was mainly wooden with iron-tyred wheels; early versions had no brakes.
- Riders often used pavements because roads were rutted; several cities (e.g. Milan, London, New York, Calcutta) restricted them.
- Pedal velocipedes appeared in the 1860s; the chain-driven safety bicycle in the 1880s.
