## Fact check

- Consumer IR remotes use infrared LEDs, typically around 940 nm, beyond the visible red limit (about 700 nm).
- Each command is encoded as a pattern of pulses (protocols such as NEC/RC-5), modulated on a carrier usually 36-40 kHz, most often 38 kHz.
- TV receivers are tuned to the carrier frequency, which lets them reject steady ambient light.
- Many phone camera sensors (especially front cameras) pick up near-infrared light, making the LED visibly flash on screen.
- Common IR remote carrier is 38 kHz: OK. Source: https://phidgets.com/docs/IR_Remote_Control_Guide
