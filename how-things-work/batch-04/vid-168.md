## Fact check

- Friction brakes convert the car's kinetic energy into heat (regenerative braking in EVs/hybrids is a separate system; the hook refers to the friction brakes).
- Disc brakes: a caliper with hydraulic pistons squeezes pads onto a rotor that turns with the wheel. Most cars have discs at the front; many also at the rear.
- Road car discs can reach a few hundred °C under repeated hard braking (about 500 °C used as an 'up to' figure); racing carbon discs run around 1,000 °C and glow.
- Vented discs have internal vanes to pump cooling air.
- Pads are normally replaced more often than discs.
- Disc temperatures: road discs reach hundreds of C only under severe braking, race discs about 1,000 C: label SOFTENED to 'Hard braking'. Source: https://flowracers.com/blog/how-hot-f1-brakes/
