## Fact check

- Soldering iron tips are a copper core (excellent heat conductor) plated with iron, often with chrome on the shank; the iron plating stops molten tin dissolving the copper.
- Typical working tip temperatures are about 300-400 °C, so 'around 350' is fair.
- Common lead-free solders (tin-silver-copper) melt at about 217-221 °C; tin-lead eutectic melts at 183 °C. 'Near two twenty' refers to lead-free.
- Temperature-controlled stations use a sensor near the tip and switch or modulate heater power to hold a set temperature.
