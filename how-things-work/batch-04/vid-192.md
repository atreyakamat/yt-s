## Fact check

- Household cling film is a thin PVC or polyethylene film (typically around 10 micrometres), much thinner than a human hair (about 50-100 micrometres).
- It has no adhesive layer; its cling comes from static charge picked up when unrolled plus close contact of the very flexible film with surfaces (some films add a tacky additive).
- Static is pulled toward nearby surfaces, the same effect as a rubbed balloon on a wall.
- Cling film often clings less well to metal containers; a commonly given explanation is that metal can conduct the charge away (softened: not firmly established).
