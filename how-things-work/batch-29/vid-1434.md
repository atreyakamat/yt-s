## Fact check

- Pump-action pressurized water blasters force water into a sealed reservoir/chamber through one-way valves, compressing the air inside.
- Compressed air stores energy like a spring; opening the trigger valve lets it drive water out of the nozzle.
- As water leaves, the air expands and pressure falls, so the stream weakens until you pump again.
- Lonnie Johnson, an engineer who worked on NASA/JPL projects, conceived the idea in the 1980s (sources give 1982 or 1985); it went on sale in 1990.
