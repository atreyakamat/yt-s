## Fact check

- Before piped supply, city dwellers drew water from wells, conduits and pumps or bought it from water carriers.
- The New River, an artificial channel bringing water from Hertfordshire to London, opened in 1613.
- Early London water mains were bored elm trunks; elm resists rot when kept permanently wet.
- Supply was intermittent (on certain days or hours), so households stored water in cisterns.
- Slow sand filtration was introduced in London by James Simpson at the Chelsea Waterworks in 1829; chlorination of public supplies began in the early 1900s.
- New River opened 1613; elm water pipes in London: OK. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_River_(England)
