## Fact check

- US Air Mail service began in 1918; early pilots navigated visually, often following railways.
- In February 1921 a transcontinental airmail flight flew through the night guided partly by bonfires lit along the route.
- The Transcontinental Airway System built lighted beacons on towers; first night airmail flights ran in 1924 and the lit New York-San Francisco airway was complete in 1925.
- Many beacons had large concrete arrows beneath them, painted (often yellow) to point along the route; some survive today.
- Radio navigation ranges replaced them from the late 1920s-1930s.
- First night airmail flights July 1924; lit NY-San Francisco airway completed 1925 (FIXED timeline from 1924/1921 to 1924/1925); US airmail began 15 May 1918: OK. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcontinental_Airway_System and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_mail
- Bonfires lit by farmers: widely reported for 1921 test flights, not confirmed in fetched sources.
