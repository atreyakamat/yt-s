## Fact check

- Bubbles in carbonated drinks rise and rest at the surface under a thin liquid film, which drains and ruptures.
- After rupture, the open cavity collapses; converging flow produces an upward jet that breaks into droplets (studied with high-speed imaging by Liger-Belair and others for champagne).
- The process takes a tiny fraction of a second, and droplets can rise several centimetres, carrying aroma compounds and producing the tickle above a glass.
