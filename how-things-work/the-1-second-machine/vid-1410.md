## Fact check

- Breaking a circuit under load produces an arc as contacts separate: the small gap and voltage ionise the air, which conducts briefly (milliseconds) and glows, like lightning on a tiny scale.
- Inductive loads (motors, transformers) resist changes in current and produce a voltage spike when interrupted, increasing arcing.
- Arcing erodes and pits contacts; switches use snap-action springs to separate contacts quickly, and switching off before unplugging avoids arcing at the plug.
