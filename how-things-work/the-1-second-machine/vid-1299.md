## Fact check

- Mass-produced cardboard jigsaw puzzles are made by laminating a printed image to board, then cutting with a steel-rule die in a press.
- The die is thin steel rule bent into the piece outlines and mounted in a board; one press stroke cuts all pieces at once.
- Pieces are then separated, mixed and boxed.
- Manufacturers commonly reuse dies, so different images can share identical piece shapes.
- Early puzzles (1760s, John Spilsbury) were wooden maps cut by hand; the 'jigsaw' name comes from the saws later used to cut wooden puzzles.
