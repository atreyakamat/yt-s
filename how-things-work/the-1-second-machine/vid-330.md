## Fact check

- Glow-starter fluorescent circuits: a neon glow discharge in the starter heats a bimetallic strip, which closes and lets current preheat the tube's filaments.
- When the strip cools and opens, the inductive ballast produces a high-voltage pulse that strikes the arc; if it fails, the cycle repeats, causing the familiar flicker.
- Starting typically takes around a second or a few seconds. Modern electronic ballasts start tubes without a separate starter.
