## Fact check

- Pinball flippers are driven by solenoids: energizing the coil pulls a steel plunger, which is linked to the flipper crank.
- Traditional flipper coils have a high-power winding for the stroke and a low-power hold winding switched in by an end-of-stroke switch; modern machines achieve the same with pulse-width control.
- This prevents the coil overheating when a flipper is held up.
- A return spring brings the flipper back when the button is released.
- A flipper stroke takes on the order of a few tens of milliseconds; a blink is roughly 100-400 ms.
