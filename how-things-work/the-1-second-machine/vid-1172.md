## Fact check

- Thermal inkjet print heads have hundreds of nozzles with diameters of tens of micrometres (smaller than a hair).
- A thin-film resistor heats ink to roughly 300 °C within microseconds, forming a vapour bubble that ejects a droplet; the bubble collapses and capillary action refills the chamber.
- Firing frequencies are typically in the tens of kilohertz (over 10,000 per second) per nozzle.
- Some inkjets (piezoelectric) push ink with a flexing crystal instead, hence 'many inkjets' in the description.
