## Fact check

- Friction between materials transfers electrons (triboelectric charging); wool and synthetics charge readily.
- Low humidity reduces charge leakage, so shocks are worse in dry winter air.
- People generally start to feel an electrostatic discharge at around 2,000-3,000 volts; body voltages can reach well over 10,000 V.
- ESD sparks last nanoseconds to around a microsecond; the snap is rapidly heated, expanding air.
- Touching metal with a key moves the spark to the key: common tip. Currents are tiny, so these shocks are harmless.
