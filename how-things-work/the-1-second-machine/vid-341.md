## Fact check

- A typical kettle whistle has two parallel plates with aligned holes forming a short chamber.
- A 2013 University of Cambridge study (Henrywood and Agarwal) found that above a certain flow speed, the steam jet sheds vortices at the second hole, producing pressure pulses heard as the whistle.
- Whistle pitches are in the low thousands of hertz, so 'thousands of times a second' fits. Pitch rises with steam flow.
