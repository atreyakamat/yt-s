## Fact check

- Lightning protection: air terminal (rod), down conductors (copper or aluminium tape) and earth electrodes.
- Rods don't attract strikes from far away; they intercept strikes that would hit the structure and provide a low-impedance path.
- Typical peak current of a lightning stroke is about 30 kA (range a few kA to 200+ kA).
- Return strokes last tens of microseconds, well under a millisecond.
- Masonry can shatter from rapid heating/steam when current passes through it.
- Franklin proposed lightning rods around 1750; kite experiment traditionally dated 1752.
