# vid-1549 · Why ice floats on water

## Hook

- **Chosen (0-3s):** "Ice floats, and that's quite unusual."
- Backup 1: "If ice sank, lakes would freeze solid."
- Backup 2: "Water breaks a basic rule of materials."

## Fact check (verify before posting)

Ice Ih density ~0.917 g/cm3 vs water ~1.000 (about 8-9% less dense). Water is densest at ~4 °C; lakes freeze from the surface down. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Properties_of_water

## Voiceover (read-through)

1. Ice floats, and that's quite unusual.
2. Most solids are denser than their liquids.
3. Water is different: ice is about nine percent less dense.
4. Freezing locks water into an open hexagonal lattice.
5. So ice floats and lakes freeze from the top.
6. The water underneath stays liquid, so fish survive.
