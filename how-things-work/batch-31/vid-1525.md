# vid-1525 · Where champagne bubbles come from

## Hook

- **Chosen (0-3s):** "Champagne bubbles aren't random. They start on specks."
- Backup 1: "Why a clean glass goes flat."
- Backup 2: "The tiny specks that make champagne fizz."

## Fact check (verify before posting)

Bubbles nucleate on cellulose fibres/imperfections in the glass; CO2 from secondary fermentation (Liger-Belair, 'Uncorked'). Hedge 'tiny fibres and scratches'.

## Voiceover (read-through)

1. Champagne bubbles aren't random. They start on specks.
2. Wine makers trap carbon dioxide gas inside during fermentation.
3. The gas stays dissolved until the pressure drops.
4. Tiny fibres and scratches on the glass give bubbles a place to form.
5. Each bubble grows as it rises, then bursts at the top.
6. That string of bubbles is the fizz you see.
