# vid-1514 · Why January 1 is the start of the year

## Hook

- Backup 1: "Why the year doesn't start on the solstice."
- Backup 2: "The god of doors started your year."

## Fact check (verify before posting)

Roman civil year began Jan 1 from 153 BC for consular term start; Julian calendar (46-45 BC) fixed it. Janus is the namesake. Confirm dates with Britannica/Oxford Classical Dictionary. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_calendar

## Voiceover (read-through)

1. January 1 is a choice, not nature.
2. Nothing in space marks January 1 as special.
3. Early Roman years began in March.
4. In 45 BC, Julius Caesar's calendar made January 1 the official start.
5. January was named after Janus, the two-faced god of doors.
6. A door looks back and forward, which suits a new year.
