## Fact check

- Electron drift velocity in household copper wire at typical currents is on the order of 0.01-0.1 mm/s; a garden snail moves around 1 mm/s.
- With AC at 50/60 Hz, electrons oscillate back and forth by a tiny distance rather than travelling along the wire.
- The signal/energy propagates as an electromagnetic field at a large fraction of the speed of light (typically about 50-99% in cables).
- The marble analogy is a standard teaching illustration.
- Electron drift speed in copper wire is a fraction of a millimetre per second: OK. Source: https://www.scienceabc.com/nature/what-is-the-speed-of-electricity.html
