## Fact check

- Plasterboard (drywall) is a gypsum core faced with paper on both sides.
- Gypsum is calcium sulfate dihydrate (CaSO4·2H2O); the bound water is about 20.9% of its mass.
- When heated, the water is released (calcination), absorbing energy; the unexposed side stays at roughly 100 °C until the water is driven off, which is why gypsum board slows fire spread.
- Fire-rated boards (e.g. Type X) add glass fibres and other additives to keep the core intact longer.
