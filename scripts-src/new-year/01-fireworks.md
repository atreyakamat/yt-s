## 1502 | Why blue fireworks are the hardest to make
hook: Blue is the hardest colour in fireworks. | BLUE IS THE *HARD ONE* | hero: fireworks Blue
- Blue comes from copper compounds, and they are fragile. | COPPER = *BLUE* | hero: atom Copper
- Too much heat and the blue-emitting molecule breaks apart. | TOO HOT, NO *BLUE* | pair: thermometer Too hot; cross Breaks
- Too cool and it just fizzles out dim. | TOO COOL, NO *GLOW* | pair: snowflake Too cool; cloud Dim
- So makers must hit a narrow temperature window. | A *NARROW* WINDOW | bars: flame Hot=3; thermometer Just right=1; snowflake Cool=2
- That's why a crisp blue shell is the one pros brag about. | THE *PRO* COLOUR | hero: star Crisp blue
fact: Copper(I) chloride emits blue but decomposes at high temperature; narrow stable range. Source: chemistry of pyrotechnic colours (ACS, Compound Interest). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrotechnic_colorant
alt: One colour keeps pyrotechnicians up at night.
alt: Why deep blue fireworks are rare.

## 1503 | Why you hear the firework after you see it
hook: You always hear fireworks after you see them. | SEE FIRST, *HEAR* LATER | pair: eye See; speaker Hear
- Light travels almost instantly across the sky. | LIGHT IS *INSTANT* | hero: bolt Light
- Sound is far slower, about 340 metres every second. | 340 M *PER SECOND* | num: 340 | metres per second | speaker
- So the further away you stand, the bigger the gap. | BIGGER *GAP* | flow: eye See; hourglass Wait; speaker Hear
- Count the seconds between flash and boom. | COUNT THE *SECONDS* | hero: clock Count
- Divide by three and that's roughly how many kilometres away it burst. | SECONDS ÷ 3 = *KM* | num: ÷ 3 | seconds to km | clock
fact: Speed of sound in air ~343 m/s at 20C, so ~3 s per km. Same rule as lightning/thunder. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_of_sound
alt: Count the seconds and you can measure the sky.
alt: Your ears are slower than your eyes.

## 1505 | The two explosions inside every firework shell
hook: Every firework shell explodes twice. | EXPLODES *TWICE* | num: 2 | explosions | burst
- First, a small charge at the bottom launches it skyward. | LAUNCH *CHARGE* | flow: box Lift charge; rocket Launch
- A slow-burning fuse ticks while it climbs. | THE *FUSE* TICKS | hero: hourglass Fuse
- At the top, the fuse reaches the main charge. | MAIN *CHARGE* | hero: burst Burst charge

fact: Aerial shells use a lift charge at the base and a time fuse leading to a bursting charge at the centre. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunpowder

## 1506 | Why fireworks burst in a perfect sphere
hook: Why do fireworks burst in perfect balls? | WHY A *PERFECT BALL* | hero: burst Sphere
- Inside the shell, stars are packed evenly around a central charge. | PACKED *EVENLY* | hero: star Stars around centre
- When it blasts, every star gets pushed with the same force. | SAME *PUSH* | grid: star Star; star Star; star Star; star Star
- Equal push in every direction makes a growing sphere. | A GROWING *SPHERE* | flow: target Centre; burst Blast; globe Sphere
- Gravity then slowly pulls the burning stars down. | THEN *GRAVITY* | hero: arrow-right Down
- That's what makes the glittering willow drop. | THE *WILLOW* DROP | hero: sparkle Willow
fact: Spherical bursts come from symmetric star placement around a bursting charge; shell construction details vary by shell type.
